When We All Suddenly Agree

Home›Columns›When We All Suddenly Agree

Why quick, unanimous consensus in a meeting sometimes means no one thought hard enough, not that the decision was obvious.

Nermin Sefić

There's a moment in a meeting I've learned to treat with suspicion instead of relief - the moment a proposal is presented, and everyone in the room immediately, without discussion, nods in agreement. Instinct says that's a good sign - a clear, obvious decision requiring no further debate. Experience has taught me the opposite is often true.

Quick, unanimous consensus sometimes genuinely reflects an obvious, good decision. But just as often it reflects group dynamics in which no one wanted to be the first person to ask a question or raise doubt, especially if the proposal was presented confidently by someone with authority in the room.

A practice I've adopted: when I notice that too-quick consensus, I deliberately slow the process down before formally closing it. "Before we move on - is there anyone who sees a risk or problem we haven't mentioned?" This simple question, asked deliberately, opens space for voices that would otherwise stay quiet, and surprisingly often reveals a concern that deserves serious consideration before the decision is formally made.
