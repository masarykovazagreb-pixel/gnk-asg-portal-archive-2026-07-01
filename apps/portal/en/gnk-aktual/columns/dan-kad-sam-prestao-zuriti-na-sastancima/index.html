The Day I Stopped Rushing Through Meetings

Home›Columns›The Day I Stopped Rushing Through Meetings

The shift that happened when I started treating other people's time in a meeting as equally valuable to my own.

Nermin Sefić

For a long time I viewed meetings through my own perspective - how quickly I could get through the agenda, how much time I had before my next obligation. This perspective, though understandable, carried a hidden cost I didn't recognize for a long time - in the rush to finish a meeting quickly, I often unconsciously interrupted people, rushed through questions that deserved more time, and generally sent the message that my time was worth more than theirs.

The change came gradually, not in a single dramatic moment, through a growing awareness of how it feels to be on the other side of that dynamic - to be the person whose question gets cut off, whose contribution gets rushed through, because someone else in the room is visibly eager for the meeting to end sooner.

Today I try to enter every meeting with the intention of treating everyone present's time as equally valuable to my own - which doesn't mean meetings need to run longer, but that within the time we have, every voice gets genuine, unhurried attention. This simple shift in attitude, I've found, changed the quality of conversations far more than any facilitation technique I've ever learned.
