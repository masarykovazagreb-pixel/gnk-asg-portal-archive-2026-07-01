Cibona Is Croatian Champion. Twenty-First Time, and This One We'll Remember

For the twenty-first time in the club's history, Cibona is champion of Croatia. Not on paper, not easily — in a decisive fifth game of the finals series, on the road at Zadar's Visnjik arena, in front of a full house cheering for the home team. The Wolves won 82:75, ending Zadar's run of three straight titles.

This was exactly the kind of game a fan wants to remember. Zadar led for long stretches, and Cibona waited for its moment, taking control in the closing minutes — with the gap down to just four points in the final seconds, the team didn't fold. That's the difference between a team that wanted to win and a team that knew how.

Justin Roberson, Zan Mark Sisko, Luka Skoric and Jan Palokaj carried the weight of the whole series, and it was exactly them deciding possessions in the decisive moments of game five. Krešimir Radovčić said something after the game that speaks louder than any statistic — that the team started with just the six of them at the first training session in late August, and that across the entire year there wasn't a single incident, not one argument. When someone builds an atmosphere like that, the title arrives as a consequence, not a coincidence.

It's especially worth noting that this win came in a season when the club was going through a major transformation off the court — the restructuring into a joint-stock company under Victor Dodig's leadership. Sporting success in the middle of organizational change isn't common, and that says even more about the character of this generation of players and staff.

This was a game to remember, and a season to remember. The Wolves showed they know how, and that they can — twenty-one times now, and this time with a story worth retelling.
