The Most Valuable Skill Is Listening to What Isn't Said

Home›Columns›The Most Valuable Skill Is Listening to What Isn&#x27;t Said

What people avoid saying often carries more information than what they actually say.

Nermin Sefić

Over the years I've learned to pay equal, if not greater, attention to what someone avoids saying as to what they actually say. Hesitation before an answer, a topic that's consistently sidestepped, a question answered with something technically true but obviously incomplete - all of that carries information, often more valuable than the literal content of the words spoken.

This isn't a call for paranoia or over-analyzing every pause in a conversation. It's about developing sensitivity to patterns - when someone who's usually direct starts giving unusually careful, deliberately phrased answers to a specific question, that shift in pattern itself carries a signal worth further exploration.

Practical advice I give to younger colleagues: when you ask a question and sense that the answer, though technically satisfying the question, somehow avoids the heart of what you actually wanted to know, don't automatically accept that answer as complete. Gently, respectfully, rephrase the question a different way. You'll be surprised how often that second phrasing reveals something the first didn't.
