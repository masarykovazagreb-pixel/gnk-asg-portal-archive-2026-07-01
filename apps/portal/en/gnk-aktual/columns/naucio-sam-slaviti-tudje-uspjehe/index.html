I Learned to Celebrate Others' Successes Louder Than My Own

Home›Columns›I Learned to Celebrate Others&#x27; Successes Louder Than My Own

The shift from focusing on my own achievements toward actively highlighting and celebrating the successes of others within the team.

Nermin Sefić

A natural instinct, especially early in a career, is to seek credit for one's own achievements - to make sure others know who stood behind a successful project or decision. This instinct, though understandable, I recognized over time as a limiting way of thinking about my own role.

The change came gradually, through observing that the strongest leaders I've known weren't the ones who most loudly highlighted their own successes, but those who actively, visibly celebrated their teams' successes, sometimes more loudly than they would ever celebrate their own. This practice wasn't modesty for its own sake - it was a strategic, deliberate investment in team motivation and loyalty.

Today I actively seek opportunities to highlight others' successes, sometimes at the expense of my own visibility. The result, surprisingly, wasn't less recognition for me personally, but the opposite - a team that feels their contributions are genuinely seen and valued naturally becomes more loyal, more motivated, and consequently more successful in ways that ultimately reflect positively on the leadership as a whole.
