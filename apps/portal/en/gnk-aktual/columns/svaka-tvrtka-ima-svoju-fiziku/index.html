Every Company Has Its Own Physics

Home›Columns›Every Company Has Its Own Physics

Why solutions that work perfectly in one organization often completely fail in another, seemingly similar one.

Nermin Sefić

How many times have I heard, and said myself, the sentence "it worked great at that other company, why wouldn't it work for us?" The answer, which I've learned through my own, sometimes costly experiences, is that every organization has something I'd call its own physics - a set of invisible rules, history, relationships, and habits that shape whether a given solution will actually work, regardless of how well it works elsewhere.

A solution that works perfectly in an organization with a flat hierarchy and fast decision-making can completely fail in an organization with a deeper hierarchy and slower, more consensus-driven decision-making - not because the solution is bad, but because it assumes a physics that doesn't exist in the new context.

This realization isn't a call to dismiss other people's experiences or ideas - I still believe we learn a lot by watching what others do. It's about adding an extra step before adoption - not "did this work there," but "why did this work there, and do those same conditions exist for us, or do we need to adapt the approach to our own, specific physics?"
