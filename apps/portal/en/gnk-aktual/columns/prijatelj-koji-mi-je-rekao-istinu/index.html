The Friend Who Told Me the Truth When I Didn't Want to Hear It

Home›Columns›The Friend Who Told Me the Truth When I Didn

The value of relationships where someone has enough courage and enough care to say what you don't want to hear.

Nermin Sefić

I have a friend who, at a moment when I was deeply convinced I was right about a decision, simply told me - "I think you're wrong, and here's why." Not softened, not overly hedged. Direct, clear, with a care that was visible precisely through that directness, not despite it.

In that moment, I felt a brief defensiveness - a natural reaction to having a decision I was convinced of challenged. But that defensiveness quickly gave way to gratitude, because I recognized how rare that act of directness really is. Most people, even close friends, avoid this kind of direct challenge, choosing instead either silence or a softened, less clear version of the same concern.

This episode taught me to recognize and value people willing to risk short-term discomfort for the long-term benefit of direct, honest feedback. Today I actively nurture such relationships, and try to be that same kind of friend to others - not always pleasant, but consistently honest, in a way that, I hope, others recognize as an expression of genuine care, not criticism for its own sake.
