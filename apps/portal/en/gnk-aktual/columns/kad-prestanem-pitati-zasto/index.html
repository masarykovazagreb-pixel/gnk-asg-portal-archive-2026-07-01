When I Stop Asking Why

Home›Columns›When I Stop Asking Why

The dangerous moment when a practice becomes "that's how we've always done it" instead of a conscious, deliberate choice.

Nermin Sefić

There's a subtle moment in the life of every practice, process, or habit within an organization - a moment when it stops being an active choice, and simply becomes "that's how we do things." This transition is rarely marked by a clear line; it happens gradually, imperceptibly, until one day you realize no one in the room actually knows why something is done a certain way, only that it's always been done that way.

This moment is dangerous because a practice that has lost its "why" becomes resistant to change in a way that has nothing to do with the actual value of that practice. People defend the status quo not because they've reconsidered and concluded it's still the best approach, but because changing the status quo is psychologically harder than simply continuing with what's already familiar.

A practice I try to maintain, though it requires discipline: periodically, deliberately asking "why do we do this this way" even for practices that seem completely standard and beyond question. Sometimes the answer confirms the practice is still correct. Sometimes it reveals that the reason that originally existed is no longer relevant, and the practice simply continued out of inertia.
