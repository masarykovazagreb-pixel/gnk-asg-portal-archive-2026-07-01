The Best Mentor Never Knew He Was a Mentor

How the most valuable lessons about leadership often came from people who never formally took on that role.

Nermin Sefić

When I think about the people who most shaped my understanding of leadership, surprisingly few of them carried the formal title of mentor. They were colleagues, sometimes people below me in the hierarchy, whose everyday decisions and reactions, watched carefully over time, taught me more than any formal mentorship program.

One such example - a colleague who never sought credit for other people's ideas, even when he easily could have, simply because that's how he chose to work, not because anyone formally taught him to. Watching him over the years, I learned more about integrity in leadership than from any book on the subject.

This realization changed how I look at developing my own team - instead of relying exclusively on formal mentorship programs, I try to create opportunities for people to spend time watching each other in practice, because some of the most valuable lessons are transmitted through exactly that kind of informal, prolonged observation, not through structured formal mentorship sessions.
