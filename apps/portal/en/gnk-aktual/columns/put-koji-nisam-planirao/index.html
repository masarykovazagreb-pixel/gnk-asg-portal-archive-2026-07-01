The Path I Didn't Plan

How some of the most important opportunities in a career come precisely from moments when the original plan fell through.

Nermin Sefić

When I look back at the key moments that shaped my professional path, surprisingly few of them were the result of an original, carefully constructed plan. More often they were moments when that plan failed - an opportunity that didn't materialize, a project that didn't succeed - which unplanned, redirected me toward a path that, in retrospect, proved more valuable than the original intention.

This realization doesn't diminish the value of planning - I still believe thoughtful planning beats simply waiting for things to sort themselves out. It's about a different relationship with failed plans - not as pure failure to be mourned, but as a potential opportunity to discover a path the original plan, in its narrowness, might never have revealed.

This perspective requires a certain kind of openness that doesn't develop automatically - the ability to actually look at what a failed plan opened up, instead of staying fixated exclusively on what was lost. People who succeed in developing that openness, I've noticed over years of watching different careers, often end up building paths richer and more interesting than any single original plan they lost.
