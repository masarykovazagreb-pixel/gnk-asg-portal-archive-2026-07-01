The Best Questions Come From Outside

The value of questions asked by people outside the organization, precisely because they don't know the assumptions no one inside questions anymore.

Nermin Sefić

Some of the most useful questions I've ever received about my own business didn't come from longtime employees or experienced industry advisors, but from people completely outside the context - a friend working in an entirely different industry, an acquaintance hearing an explanation of how we do something for the first time. Their questions, sometimes naively phrased, often cut through assumptions that have become so deeply embedded within the organization that no one sees them as assumptions anymore, just as simple facts.

"Why do you even do it that way?" - a question that would sound almost insultingly naive coming from someone inside the organization, since it implies they don't understand the "obvious" reasons, becomes genuinely useful when asked by someone outside, precisely because they don't have access to those same, perhaps outdated assumptions that internal voices no longer question.

A practice I've adopted: occasionally, deliberately seeking the chance to explain my own business to someone completely outside the context, not for their advice, but for the questions they'll naturally ask during that explanation. Surprisingly often, exactly those questions reveal assumptions worth reconsidering.
