The Most Important Conversation I Keep Postponing

The pattern of postponing exactly the conversations that matter most, and the real cost of that postponement.

Nermin Sefić

I've noticed a pattern in myself that I don't think is unique - the conversations I postpone most are almost always exactly the most important ones. An easy conversation about a routine matter, I handle right away. A hard conversation - about performance that isn't up to standard, about a decision that will disappoint someone, about a problem requiring uncomfortable honesty - that conversation finds a way to get pushed to "next week."

The rationalization is always available - "I'm not quite ready yet," "I need more information before the conversation," "maybe the situation will resolve itself." Sometimes that rationalization is true. More often it's simply avoidance of discomfort, dressed in the language of prudence.

The cost of this postponement is rarely immediately visible, which makes it particularly dangerous. The problem the conversation would resolve continues to simmer, sometimes growing, until the conversation finally happens - usually too late, under greater pressure, with more accumulated frustration than if it had happened when it was first needed.

A discipline I try to build: when I notice I'm postponing something, ask myself whether it's because I genuinely need more information, or because I'm avoiding discomfort. If it's the latter, that's a sign this conversation is exactly the one that needs to happen next, not last on the list.
