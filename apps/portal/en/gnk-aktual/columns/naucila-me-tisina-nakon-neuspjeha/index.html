What I Learned From the Silence After Failure

What a team's silence after a failure reveals about the real psychological safety within an organization.

Nermin Sefić

After one significant project failure, I called a meeting expecting an open, honest analysis of what went wrong. Instead, I was met with almost complete silence - short, cautious answers, no one willing to take the initiative in explaining what happened. That silence, more than any verbal statement, revealed to me the real state of psychological safety within the team.

People don't stay silent because they have nothing to say. They stay silent when they judge that speaking might be punished, directly or subtly. That silence was a clear, if uncomfortable, signal that our declared culture of "open communication about mistakes" wasn't an actual practice the team genuinely believed it could safely apply.

This episode forced me into deep reconsideration of my own behavior in previous situations - had I, perhaps unconsciously, through subtle signals, punished previous attempts at openly admitting mistakes? The answer, after honest self-reflection, was probably yes. Fixing this dynamic took months of consistent, visible modeling of different behavior before the team began to believe again that openness was genuinely safe.
