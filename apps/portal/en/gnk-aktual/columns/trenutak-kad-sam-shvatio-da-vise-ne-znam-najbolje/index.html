The Moment I Realized I No Longer Know Best

Home›Columns›The Moment I Realized I No Longer Know Best

Accepting that younger team members sometimes understand certain areas better than I do.

Nermin Sefić

There's a moment in every leader's career when they first confront the realization that a younger colleague, with less formal experience but a deeper, fresher understanding of a specific area, genuinely knows more about that particular topic than they do. For me, that moment came surprisingly uncomfortably, despite rationally knowing it's a completely reasonable, almost inevitable consequence of the speed at which many fields change today.

My first instinct was mild defensiveness - a need to prove that my broader, accumulated experience still carried value, even in an area where a younger colleague had deeper, more specific knowledge. Later I recognized that this defensiveness, if indulged, prevents exactly the kind of learning that would benefit me most.

Today I actively seek opportunities to learn from younger team members in areas where they genuinely know more, treating it as a valuable opportunity, not a threat to my own authority. This shift in attitude not only allows me continuous learning, but also models the behavior I want to see throughout the team - a humility stable enough to admit when someone else knows more, regardless of formal hierarchy.
