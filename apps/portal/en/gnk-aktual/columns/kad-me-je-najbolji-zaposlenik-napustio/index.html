When My Best Employee Left

Home›Columns›When My Best Employee Left

A lesson learned when one of the best employees resigned, and why the reason was harder to hear than any criticism.

Nermin Sefić

When one of our best people announced they were leaving, my first instinct was to ask what we could offer to keep them - more pay, a different position. The answer I got was harder to hear than any offer I could have refused - "it's not about the money. I feel like my work here is no longer seen."

That sentence hit me because I honestly believed we valued his work - we said so, from time to time, in annual reviews. But apparently that occasional, formal confirmation wasn't enough to make up for the daily, concrete visibility of his contribution in regular, ongoing work.

This lesson changed how I approach recognizing contributions - I realized that an annual review, however carefully written, can't substitute for consistent, everyday visibility. Today I try to recognize good work the moment it happens, not wait for a formal opportunity months later - a lesson I learned the hard way, by losing someone who deserved better than what I, despite good intentions, was actually giving him.
