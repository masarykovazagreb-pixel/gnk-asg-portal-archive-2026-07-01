The Best Negotiator Isn't Always the Loudest

Home›Columns›The Best Negotiator Isn

A lesson learned watching a negotiator who won through patience and preparation, not by dominating the room.

Nermin Sefić

Early in my career I watched a negotiator I was certain would lose - he spoke quietly, rarely raised his voice, left long pauses before answering while the other side energetically pressed its arguments. By the end of the negotiation, that same person got nearly everything he'd asked for, while the louder side walked away feeling they'd "won," despite getting considerably less than they'd originally demanded.

The secret, as I understood later, wasn't passivity or weakness - it was deep preparation and patience that the louder side simply didn't have. While the other side rushed toward a quick conclusion, the quieter negotiator patiently waited for moments when the other side's haste would reveal information or weaknesses that could be used, without ever raising his tone or showing urgency.

This lesson changed my own approach to negotiation - I gave up trying to "win" through volume or dominating the room, and started investing considerably more in preparation before the negotiation itself, and patience during it. The results have consistently been better than any earlier, louder strategy I'd previously tried.
