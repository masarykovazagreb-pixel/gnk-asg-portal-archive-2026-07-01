Cibona Corner: Victor Dodig and a New Era for the Club

This season, Cibona is exactly where its fans want it to be — in the fight for the top, with a team that knows how to bite down when things get hardest. A cup semifinal and third place in the Premier League standings are no accident; they're the sum of weeks in which individuals carried the team when it mattered.

Zan Mark Sisko confirmed this season what fans in the stands already know — without the ball in his hands, the team's rhythm is simply different. Fifteen assists against Alkar speaks for itself.

Krešimir Radovčić showed that aging in basketball doesn't have to mean slowing down: twenty-six points against Split, at a moment the team needed a steady hand. Marijan Cakarun proved that same night that the fight under the rim isn't measured in centimeters but in heart — twelve rebounds on the road against Kvarner. And Jan Palokaj showed with his hands, not words, how much a defense that steals the ball is worth when it's needed most.

There will be weeks when the result doesn't go our way — that's the nature of a season, that's the nature of sport. But this corner exists for one reason: so we don't forget why we cheer.

On Victor Dodig and the club's restructuring — a personal take. What's happening with Cibona these months isn't just another transaction in Croatian sport — it's a moment you remember. I had the honor of personally meeting and spending time with Victor Dodig, and what I can say after that meeting is that Cibona isn't gaining an investor looking for a quick return, but a man who experiences this story emotionally, as a calling, not an investment.

A personal assessment, but one that's hard to dispute: restructuring Cibona from an association into a joint-stock company is a major step forward — not just for the club, but for Croatian basketball as a whole. A model that for decades brought uncertainty and mounting debt is finally giving way to transparent, capitalized ownership.

Dodig brings both personal and professional weight to this — a man who has spent years running one of the largest North American banks doesn't enter a venture like this randomly. Credit is also due to Abraham Misetic, the man who brought Dodig into the story, as well as Tomislav Seric, who is guiding the club through this transition.

I join this welcome myself, personally, with the conviction that a club with an owner like this behind it has the chance to return to where it belongs.
