Data Without an Owner Is Not an Asset

Home›Commentary›Data Without an Owner Is Not an Asset

Data no one formally owns doesn't get maintained, doesn't get verified, and over time becomes a risk instead of a value.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

Data no one formally owns doesn't get maintained, doesn't get verified, and over time becomes a risk instead of a value.

Every dataset needs a named owner responsible for its accuracy, security, and purpose of use — without that, the data exists but no one actually maintains it. GNK ASG d.o.o. assigns clear responsibility for every publicly published data source before it's used in analytics or reporting.

A dataset without a clearly assigned person responsible for its accuracy, currency, and availability gradually loses value — no one corrects errors, no one updates stale records, because formally no one is accountable for it.

Assigning a data owner is not a bureaucratic step but a precondition for treating data as an asset at all — because an asset, by definition, has someone looking after its upkeep and value.

In practice, this means every report, table, or dashboard should have a clearly named person accountable for its accuracy — not a department, but an individual who knows where the data comes from and how it's updated.

Assigning a clear data owner is not a bureaucratic formality but a precondition for treating data as an asset at all, since an asset by definition requires someone accountable for maintaining its value.

In practice, assigning ownership means naming a specific person accountable for a dataset's accuracy and currency, not merely a department or system — accountability diffused across a team tends, in practice, to belong to no one in particular, which produces exactly the same neglect as having no owner at all.
