Resilience Is Not Visible Until It's Actually Tested

A business continuity plan that has never been run through a real drill remains just a document, not an actual capability.

Nermin Sefić30 July 2026

The existence of a written plan for crisis scenarios doesn't guarantee an organisation will actually know how to respond when the scenario happens — the difference between a document and a real capability only shows through an actual drill or a genuine incident. The GNK DINAMO Ltd. Group regularly tests its own continuity plans precisely to avoid that surprise in a real crisis.

A business continuity plan that has never been run through a real drill remains just a document, not an actual capability.

A system that has never faced real pressure may appear stable simply because it has never been seriously tested — stability without a test is an assumption, not a proven fact.

Regular, deliberately organised resilience tests — even when they uncover uncomfortable weaknesses — are worth more than the false sense of security that comes from the mere absence of problems so far.

The gap between a plan that exists on paper and a capability that genuinely exists inside the organisation is visible only to those who have actually verified that capability before the crisis arrives.
