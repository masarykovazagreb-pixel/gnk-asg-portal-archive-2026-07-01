The Line Between Suggestion and Decision Must Stay Visible

When internal communication loses the distinction between what a model proposed and what a person decided, accountability becomes hard to establish.

Nermin Sefić3 August 2026

In organisations that intensively use AI tools to prepare analyses and recommendations, there is a risk that, over time, the clear line between what the system proposed and what the accountable employee actually decided gets lost.

Consistently labelling every AI-generated suggestion as such, until it is formally confirmed by the accountable person, maintains a clear line of accountability even when the model was used at an early stage of decision preparation.

When a system automatically turns a suggestion into a recorded decision without a clear step of human confirmation, that boundary gradually fades from the user's awareness, until they stop meaningfully questioning what has been proposed to them.

A visible boundary means the interface clearly distinguishes 'this is a recommendation' from 'this is the decision you made' — a difference that looks cosmetic but directly determines whether anyone actually re-examines a result or simply confirms it by reflex.
