Technology Integration Speed Does Not Replace Testing

A system launched before it has been thoroughly tested usually ends up tested live, at the expense of customers who never asked for that.

Nermin Sefić1 August 2026

A system launched before it has been thoroughly tested usually ends up tested live, at the expense of customers who never asked for that.

The pressure to deploy new technology ahead of competitors often shortens testing precisely when it should be most thorough, since the cost of a production error always exceeds the cost of a delayed launch. GNK ASG d.o.o. separates the innovation phase from the production-stability phase precisely because of that difference in risk.

The visible cost of delaying a launch is a few predictable weeks. The cost of a production error after skipped testing is rarely visible in advance, and almost always larger once it materialises through downtime, data loss, or eroded user trust.

A clear boundary between development and production environments is therefore not a bureaucratic obstacle to innovation but a precondition for innovation to be fast at all — it allows experimentation without risking the systems that daily operations depend on.

Separating the experimentation phase from the production-stability phase lets innovation move fast while keeping the risk of error confined to a controlled environment.
