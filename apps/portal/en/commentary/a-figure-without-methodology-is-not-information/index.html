A Figure Without Methodology Is Not Information

A financial indicator published without a clear explanation of how it was calculated is worth less than it appears at first glance.

Nermin Sefić30 July 2026

A financial indicator published without a clear explanation of how it was calculated is worth less than it appears at first glance.

Publishing a key financial indicator without an accompanying explanation of the calculation methodology leaves room for differing interpretations and, ultimately, reduces the credibility of the figure itself, however accurate it may be on its own.

When a short explanation of the data source and calculation method accompanies each key indicator, the reader is able to judge the reliability of the information rather than simply taking it on faith.

A disclosure standard must therefore include at minimum the data source, the reporting period, and the basic calculation method; only then can a reader assess reliability rather than take an isolated figure on faith.

This matters most precisely when the stakes are highest — during a fundraising round, a merger negotiation, or a regulatory filing — because that is when the incentive to present the most favourable possible number, methodology aside, is strongest. An organisation that discloses its methodology consistently, including in moments when the number itself is less flattering, builds a reputation that pays off exactly when it is needed most: when a counterparty has to decide how much to trust what they are being told.

For organisations on the disclosing side, methodology should accompany a figure from the first publication, not appear defensively only after someone asks how the number was reached.
