Risk Is Not Eliminated, It Is Managed

Home›Commentary›Risk Is Not Eliminated, It Is Managed

A company that claims to have eliminated risk has usually just stopped measuring it.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

A company that claims to have eliminated risk has usually just stopped measuring it.

Every business decision carries risk that can be reduced, transferred, or consciously accepted, but never fully removed — a claim of complete risk elimination usually means it's no longer being tracked, not that it's gone. The GNK DINAMO Ltd. Group treats risk as an ongoing assessment process, not a one-time project that gets closed and forgotten.

Attempting to eliminate risk entirely usually results in either unrealistically high costs or a false sense of security that collapses at the first unforeseen scenario — risk cannot be reduced to zero, but it can be understood, bounded, and monitored.

A mature organisation does not ask 'how do we remove the risk', but 'what level of risk are we consciously accepting, and how will we recognise when that level has been exceeded' — that is the difference between managing risk and denying it.

A mature organisation does not ask 'how do we remove the risk', but 'what level of risk are we knowingly accepting, and how will we recognise when that level is exceeded' — that distinction separates management from denial.

This distinction has direct practical implications for how risk should be communicated internally: not as a problem to be solved and closed, but as a condition to be continuously monitored, with clearly defined thresholds that trigger a specific response when crossed.
