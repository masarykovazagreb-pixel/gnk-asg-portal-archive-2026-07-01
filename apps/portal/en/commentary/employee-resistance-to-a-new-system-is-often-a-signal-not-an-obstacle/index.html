Employee Resistance to a New System Is Often a Signal, Not an Obstacle

When employees avoid a new system, the cause is more often practical shortcomings in the solution than general resistance to change.

Nermin Sefić3 August 2026

When employees avoid using a newly introduced digital system or openly resist it, the cause is, in practice, far more often specific, practical shortcomings of the solution itself than general resistance to change.

Rather than interpreting resistance solely as an organisational culture problem, it is useful to view it as direct feedback on where the new system fails to meet the real needs of everyday work.

An employee who resists a new system often is the first to notice a shortcoming the system's designers missed — resistance is then not an obstacle to implementation but an early source of information worth hearing before it is dismissed as mere reluctance to change.

Organisations that systematically gather and analyse the reasons behind resistance before full rollout typically avoid more expensive fixes after deployment — the difference lies in whether signals are heard while the system can still be adjusted, or only after it is already fixed in place.
