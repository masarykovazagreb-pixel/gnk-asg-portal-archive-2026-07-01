System Integration Matters as Much as the Systems

Home›Commentary›System Integration Matters as Much as the Systems

An organisation with several excellent but poorly connected digital tools is often less effective than one with a simpler, well-integrated system.

Nermin Sefić3 August 2026

An organisation using several individually excellent but poorly connected digital tools is, in practice, often less operationally effective than one using a simpler but well-integrated system.

Integration quality, meaning the ease with which data flows between different parts of the business system, directly affects how much time is spent on manual reconciliation instead of real analytical work.

The best individual system loses much of its value if data must be manually transferred to another system, because every manual transfer introduces delay and a chance for error that an automated integration simply does not have.

Evaluating new technology cannot stop at 'is this system good on its own' — it must also ask 'what will it cost to make it talk to everything else we already use', since integration cost often exceeds the cost of the licence itself.
