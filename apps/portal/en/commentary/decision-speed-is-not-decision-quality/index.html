Decision Speed Is Not Decision Quality

Organisations that measure success by decision speed often overlook whether the decision was based on sufficiently verified information.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

Organisations that measure success by decision speed often overlook whether the decision was based on sufficiently verified information.

The pressure to decide quickly easily turns into a measure of success in its own right, regardless of whether the decision was grounded in verified information. A fast decision made without checking assumptions carries risk further into the process, where it's harder and costlier to fix than at the moment of decision.

A team that consistently makes good fast decisions almost always shares one trait: pre-agreed rules for typical situations, so that speed in the moment comes from recognising a pattern, not from skipping analysis.

Without that preparation, a fast decision is a gamble with an extra step — it looks decisive, but it simply passes uncertainty further down the process, where correcting it is always more expensive than it would have been at the outset.

An equally valuable discipline is knowing when to slow a decision down for additional verification, and when to speed it up because the information is already reliable enough.

Organisations that consistently make fast, good decisions typically invested in the underlying frameworks long before the pressure to decide quickly arrived — the speed observed in the moment is the visible output of preparation that happened earlier and less visibly.
