Energy Efficiency Is Not Just a Cost, It's Also a Risk

Organisations viewing energy efficiency purely through the lens of cost, not resilience to future price shocks, are underestimating part of the real picture.

Nermin Sefić3 August 2026

Investment in energy efficiency is often, in internal discussion, reduced solely to the question of cost and payback period, while the dimension of reduced exposure to future, hard-to-predict energy price shocks is overlooked.

An organisation less dependent on energy price volatility also has greater operational stability during periods of external disruption beyond its control.

An organisation that views energy efficiency purely as a cost-saving measure overlooks that dependence on cheap but unstable energy is itself a source of operational risk once conditions in the energy market shift.

Investment in efficiency and diversified energy sources should therefore be measured as a reduction in risk exposure, not only as a reduction in the bill — the two effects rarely align on the same timeline, but both are real.
