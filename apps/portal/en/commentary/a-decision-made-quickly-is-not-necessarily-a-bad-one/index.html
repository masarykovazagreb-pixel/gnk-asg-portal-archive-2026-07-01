A Decision Made Quickly Is Not Necessarily a Bad One

Decision speed and decision quality are often wrongly seen as opposites, though in practice both depend on preparation done before the moment of decision.

Nermin Sefić3 August 2026

Organisations that have clearly predefined criteria and boundaries for particular types of decisions can decide quickly without sacrificing quality, since most of the analytical work was done before the specific situation arose.

Slowness in decision-making is often not a sign of thoroughness, but a consequence of lacking clear, pre-agreed frameworks within which a decision can be made quickly yet responsibly.

Building the frameworks that make speed safe takes time upfront, which is precisely why organisations under constant pressure to show results often skip that step, and pay for it later.

Deliberation that happened when the framework was built lets what looks like a fast decision in the moment actually be the fast execution of a decision already made earlier, under calmer conditions.

Sooner or later, an organisation that consistently skips this preparation pays for it, one slow, high-stakes decision at a time, exactly when speed would have mattered most.

The gap between the two situations is rarely visible in the moment itself; it only becomes clear afterward, when the same decision either holds up or has to be revisited.
