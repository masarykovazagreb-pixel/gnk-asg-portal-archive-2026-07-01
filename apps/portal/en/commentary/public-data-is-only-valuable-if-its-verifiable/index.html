Public Data Is Only Valuable If It's Verifiable

Publishing a figure without a source isn't transparency — it's just a claim in a different form.

Nermin Sefić1 August 2026

Publishing a figure without a source isn't transparency — it's just a claim in a different form.

Publicly disclosing financial or operational indicators without a clear link to the source and calculation methodology isn't fundamentally different from a private claim — the only difference is who hears it. GNK ASG d.o.o. links every publicly disclosed indicator to a documented source precisely so that verifiability, not just disclosure, is the standard.

The link to the source doesn't need to be long or technically complex — it just needs to exist and lead to a place where the figure can actually be checked.

This commentary is part of the GNK ASG Intelligence Desk system and is informational in nature.

Verifiability does not require publishing every internal document; it is enough to clearly state who compiled the figure, what date it refers to, and where the methodology can be checked.

GNK ASG d.o.o. applies this standard to its own public disclosures by consistently linking figures to their documented source, treating verifiability not as an optional enhancement but as the baseline requirement for anything published as fact rather than opinion.
