Auditor Independence Matters More Than Reporting Speed

Pressure to release a financial report earlier rarely justifies concessions in the depth and independence of the audit process.

Nermin Sefić31 July 2026

Pressure to release a financial report earlier rarely justifies concessions in the depth and independence of the audit process.

When the market or investors push for faster disclosure of financial results, there's constant pressure to shorten the audit process — which can, over the long run, undermine the very trust that publishing the results was meant to build.

Pressure to complete an audit before some external deadline creates the temptation to subordinate auditor independence to speed — yet that independence is precisely the reason an audited report carries additional value in the first place.

An organisation that rushes an audit signals that form matters more to it than the substance of the review — a signal that, once recognised, is harder to undo than simply waiting for the audit to be completed properly.

Organisations that clearly communicate that audit independence and thoroughness take priority over speed of release generally build a more stable relationship with long-term investors than those who treat speed as a virtue in itself.

The pressure to compress an audit timeline rarely comes from the auditor; it comes from the organisation being audited, often for reasons that have nothing to do with the audit's substance — a financing deadline, a board meeting, a public announcement already scheduled. Recognising that the pressure originates internally, and building in the buffer time needed to avoid transmitting it to the audit process, is a discipline worth establishing well before any specific deadline creates the temptation to skip it.
