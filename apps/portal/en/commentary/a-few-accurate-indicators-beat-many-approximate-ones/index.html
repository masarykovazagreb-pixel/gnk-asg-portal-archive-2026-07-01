A Few Accurate Indicators Are Worth More Than Many Approximate Ones

A report with fifty superficial metrics rarely gives a better picture than five well-chosen, accurately measured indicators.

Nermin Sefić30 July 2026

A report with fifty superficial metrics rarely gives a better picture than five well-chosen, accurately measured indicators.

The number of tracked indicators is often mistakenly equated with the quality of oversight, while a smaller number of carefully chosen and reliably measured metrics usually gives a clearer picture of the real state of a business. GNK ASG d.o.o. deliberately limits the number of key indicators in public reporting precisely so each one retains real weight and verifiability.

A report listing fifty superficial metrics, half of which are estimates, creates an impression of thoroughness while making it harder for the reader to distinguish what has actually been verified from what is merely a best guess at the time of writing.

A smaller set of consistently measured, clearly defined indicators, updated on a reliable schedule, gives a more trustworthy picture of the business than an extensive report mixing different levels of confidence — the quality of a metric matters more than its quantity.

When the number of indicators is deliberately limited, it's also easier to ensure each one is genuinely measured accurately and reviewed regularly, rather than just updated occasionally.

The temptation to add more metrics comes from a reasonable instinct — more data should mean more insight — but that instinct breaks down once the indicators being tracked are inconsistently measured, updated on different schedules, or defined differently across departments. At that point, additional metrics add noise rather than clarity, and a reader trying to form a judgement has to first work out which numbers can actually be trusted before even beginning to interpret them. An organisation that resists the pressure to expand its reporting and instead invests in the reliability of a smaller set of indicators usually communicates more clearly, not less.
