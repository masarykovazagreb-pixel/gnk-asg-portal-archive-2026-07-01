Transparency Begins Before the Report

Transparency isn't a final document but the way data is collected, decisions are explained, and accountability is recorded throughout a process.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

Transparency isn't a final document but the way data is collected, decisions are explained, and accountability is recorded throughout a process.

A report can look tidy while the process behind it remains opaque. Transparency begins when it's known in advance what data will be collected, who verifies it, and how deviations are resolved.

Transparency that only appears at the moment an annual report is published arrives too late to build trust — it must be embedded in everyday data collection and record-keeping practice, long before anything is published.

An organisation that does not maintain orderly records throughout the year cannot suddenly become transparent simply because a reporting deadline has arrived — the report then becomes a reconstruction of memory, not a reflection of data actually kept.

A published result is only as good as the credible trail of the process that produced it.
