A Consolidated Group Report Is Not the Same as an Audited One

Home›Commentary›A Consolidated Group Report Is Not the Same as an Audited One

The difference between management-confirmed, consolidated group data and independently audited individual reports is often overlooked in public communication.

Nermin Sefić30 July 2026

The difference between management-confirmed, consolidated group data and independently audited individual reports is often overlooked in public communication.

A consolidated financial presentation of an entire group of companies and an independently audited report of a single legal entity serve different purposes and shouldn't be presented as equivalent levels of verifiability.

A consolidated group presentation is useful for understanding the overall picture, but it does not carry the same level of independent verification as an audited report of an individual company — that distinction must be clearly stated, not left for the reader to assume.

Blurring the two standards, even unintentionally, creates the impression of a higher degree of formal confirmation than actually exists — a misunderstanding that, once it takes hold, is far harder to correct than it would have been to prevent.

Clearly labelling which data is independently audited and which is an internal, management-reviewed presentation lets the reader correctly calibrate the confidence level of each individual figure.
