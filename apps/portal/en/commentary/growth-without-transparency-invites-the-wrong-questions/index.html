Growth Without Transparency Invites the Wrong Questions

Exceptionally fast revenue growth, without a clear explanation of its sources, often looks more suspicious to the market than it should, even when genuine.

Nermin Sefić3 August 2026

Extremely fast revenue or profit growth, if not accompanied by a clear and verifiable explanation of its actual sources, often looks more suspicious to the market than it objectively is, even when it reflects entirely legitimate business success.

Proactively explaining the structure and sources of rapid growth, including the key markets and partnerships that enabled it, prevents legitimate success from being interpreted through a lens of suspicion.

Fast growth without a clear explanation of its source naturally invites questions — is it sustainable, is it real, is it accompanied by adequate control — and those questions are asked with more suspicion when an organisation does not offer answers in advance.

Transparent communication about what exactly is driving growth, what its limits are, and what risks accompany it, turns potentially suspicious questions into a simple confirmation of what the organisation has already explained.
