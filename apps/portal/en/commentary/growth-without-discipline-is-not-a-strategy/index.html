Growth Without Discipline Is Not a Strategy

Growth speed without cost and risk control isn't a strategy, but a temporary state.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

Growth speed without cost and risk control isn't a strategy, but a temporary state.

Growth not accompanied by control of cost, risk, and execution quality isn't a strategy in the true sense of the word, but a temporary state dependent on favourable external circumstances. Strategy implies the ability to sustain growth even in less favourable conditions. That requires discipline in spending capital just as much as ambition in setting goals. Strategy implies the ability to sustain growth even under less favourable conditions, which requires as much discipline in spending capital as ambition in setting goals for acquiring it.

A company can grow revenue for years and still be operating on borrowed time if that growth depends on conditions it does not control — when those conditions shift, growth built without discipline reverses just as quickly as it arrived.

The question worth asking before growth accelerates, not after it stalls, is whether the organisation would slow down in a controlled way if conditions turned, or unravel because supporting systems were never built for contraction.

The GNK DINAMO Ltd. Group treats this distinction as a governance requirement, reviewing every expansion plan alongside the control capacity meant to support it, approved together or not at all.

Ambition and the ability to sustain it are treated as a single decision, not two separate questions handled independently of each other.

That review happens before expansion accelerates, not after growth has already outpaced the systems meant to support it.
