Trust Is Built Slowly and Lost Quickly

One broken agreement erases the effect of ten kept ones, because trust is measured by the worst case, not the average.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

One broken agreement erases the effect of ten kept ones, because trust is measured by the worst case, not the average.

Partners and investors don't remember the average level of reliability, but the exceptions — the moment a deadline was missed without explanation or a promise wasn't kept. That's why trust isn't built through a marketing message, but through consistency in small, verifiable steps over time.

Years of consistently meeting obligations build trust that a single broken commitment can seriously damage — that asymmetry isn't unfair, it reflects the reality that one proof of unreliability is enough to change future risk assessment.

Every organisation building long-term relationships must therefore treat each individual obligation with the same seriousness as its largest contract — because a partner who once sees unreliability in something small reasonably suspects it could appear in something large as well.
