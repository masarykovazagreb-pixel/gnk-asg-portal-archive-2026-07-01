Documentation Is Cheaper Than a Dispute

The cost of keeping tidy documentation never exceeds the cost of proving something that was never written down.

Nermin Sefić1 August 2026

The cost of keeping tidy documentation never exceeds the cost of proving something that was never written down.

Decisions, agreements, and changes not documented in writing at the moment they're made become subject to speculation exactly when it matters most to prove them — in a dispute, an audit, or a change of ownership. GNK ASG d.o.o. builds decision-documentation practice as a standard part of the business process, not an afterthought.

The cost of maintaining a clear record of decisions, agreements, and changes throughout a project is negligible compared to the cost of proving later who agreed to what, once memories naturally diverge between the parties involved.

Documentation does not need to be extensive to be useful — a date, the person who approved a change, and a brief reason are usually exactly what is missing once a disagreement turns into a dispute over who said what.

The practice of writing minutes at the moment of decision, rather than reconstructing memory afterward, is one of the cheapest investments in a company's legal and operational security.
