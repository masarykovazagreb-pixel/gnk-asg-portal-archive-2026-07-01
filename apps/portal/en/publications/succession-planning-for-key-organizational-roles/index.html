Succession Planning for Key Organizational Roles

Organisational resilience isn't measured by headcount, but by the number of critical functions that depend on exactly one person.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

Organisational resilience isn't measured by headcount, but by the number of critical functions that depend on exactly one person.

Every organisation has positions whose sudden loss would stop or significantly slow key processes — the question isn't whether such positions exist, but whether they've been identified.

A succession plan doesn't necessarily mean a pre-named replacement; it means a documented process, access rights not tied exclusively to one person, and clear accountability for knowledge transfer.

Regular assessment of critical-knowledge concentration — who is the only person who knows how something works — is cheaper in advance than after that person leaves or becomes unavailable.

The practice of documenting processes independent of the individual is part of the broader discipline of operational resilience, not just a human-resources question.

Identifying and developing internal successors well ahead of an anticipated departure reduces both the disruption risk and the cost associated with an unplanned external search.

Regular reassessment of succession candidates, as their skills and the role's requirements both evolve, keeps the plan genuinely current rather than static.

This living approach to succession planning, updated regularly rather than filed away after initial creation, keeps the organisation genuinely prepared rather than merely documented as such.

Organisations that pair succession planning with genuine development investment in identified candidates, not merely identifying potential successors on paper, build considerably more robust organisational resilience than those maintaining succession plans as a purely administrative exercise. This development investment also serves broader talent retention goals, since employees who see genuine growth pathways are more likely to remain with the organisation.
