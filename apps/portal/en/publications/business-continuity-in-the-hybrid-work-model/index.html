Business Continuity in the Hybrid Work Model

Hybrid work changes the assumptions behind business continuity plans; office location is no longer the only point of failure to cover.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

Hybrid work changes the assumptions behind business continuity plans; office location is no longer the only point of failure to cover.

Business continuity plans have traditionally been built around the physical office location as the single critical point to protect or replace in case of disruption.

The hybrid work model disperses that assumption: employees access systems from different networks, devices, and locations, so resilience now depends as much on identity, access rights, and tool availability as on the building itself.

A practical approach includes an inventory of location-independent critical functions, clear rules for temporary access loss, and regular testing of scenarios where part of the team lacks a standard work environment.

A continuity plan written before an organisation adopted hybrid work rarely accounts for the specific vulnerabilities that model introduces, making a dedicated review worthwhile even for plans otherwise considered current.

A plan that assumes employees are either fully in-office or fully remote, without a defined protocol for the more common hybrid pattern, leaves a meaningful gap in coverage precisely where most organisations now operate.

Closing that gap requires deliberately modelling the hybrid scenario, not simply extending assumptions built for one extreme or the other.

Testing the plan against a realistic hybrid scenario, not a simplified all-remote or all-office simulation, is the only way to discover whether it actually holds up in practice.

Involving employees themselves in reviewing the plan's realism often surfaces gaps that a purely top-down drafting process would overlook entirely.

Revisiting the plan whenever the organisation's hybrid policy itself changes keeps continuity planning aligned with how people are actually working, not how they worked when the plan was first written.
