Single-Supplier Concentration Risk

When a single supplier covers more than a third of a critical component, a disruption in their delivery becomes a risk to the whole operation, not just procurement.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

When a single supplier covers more than a third of a critical component, a disruption in their delivery becomes a risk to the whole operation, not just procurement.

Concentration with a single supplier often develops gradually, through individually rational decisions on price and quality, with no one tracking aggregate risk at the level of the whole supplier portfolio. The result is a company that doesn't even know its exposure until a delivery disruption happens.

Tracking the largest supplier's share by critical category, with a defined threshold above which finding an alternative is automatically triggered, turns diversification from an occasional initiative into an ongoing discipline. The cost of maintaining a second qualified supplier is almost always lower than the cost of halted production.

Tracking supplier concentration as a standing metric, reviewed alongside other risk indicators, surfaces gradual drift toward dependency before it becomes a critical vulnerability.

The cost of maintaining a second qualified supplier for critical components is almost always lower than the cost of a prolonged single-source disruption.

GNK ASG d.o.o. tracks supplier concentration as a standing risk indicator across the GNK DINAMO Ltd. Group's procurement function.

Organisations that build this diversification discipline into standard procurement practice, rather than treating it as an exceptional response to a specific concern, develop supply chains genuinely more resilient to the disruptions that periodically affect even well-managed single-source relationships. This proactive stance, while requiring modest ongoing investment, consistently proves less costly than the alternative of managing an actual supply disruption without adequate alternatives already qualified.
