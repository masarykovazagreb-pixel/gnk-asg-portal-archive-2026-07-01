Ownership Succession and Business Continuity

Ownership transfer without a pre-agreed plan remains the most common cause of continuity disruption in family and ownership-concentrated companies.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

Ownership transfer without a pre-agreed plan remains the most common cause of continuity disruption in family and ownership-concentrated companies.

The moment of ownership change — whether planned or sudden — reveals whether governance authority, signing rights, and access to key systems were ever clearly documented independent of one person.

Companies that keep ownership concentrated in one or two people rarely formally test the scenario of their sudden unavailability, even though the operational risk is comparable to any other business continuity plan.

A practical approach includes a written succession plan for key authorities, clearly defined alternate signatories, and regularly updated system and account access lists that must not depend on one person's memory.

The GNK DINAMO Ltd. Group's ownership structure is documented through public registries precisely for that reason — structural transparency makes it easier for any third party to verify who actually makes the decisions.

Beginning succession planning years before an anticipated transition, rather than when it becomes urgent, allows for the kind of gradual knowledge transfer that rushed handovers cannot replicate.

External advisors, brought in to facilitate succession planning discussions, can help navigate the emotional complexity that purely internal conversations often struggle to address.

This external facilitation proves particularly valuable in family businesses, where personal relationships can otherwise complicate objective discussion of succession timing and criteria.

Organisations that begin this planning process years in advance, treating it as an ongoing dialogue rather than a single decisive conversation, navigate the eventual transition with considerably less disruption than those addressing succession only when circumstances force the issue. This extended timeline also allows successors genuine time to develop the judgement the role ultimately requires.
