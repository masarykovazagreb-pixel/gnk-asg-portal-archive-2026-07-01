Industrial Policy: US, Europe, Asia — A New Phase

Subsidies, supply chain localisation, and technological sovereignty are reshaping the global allocation of capital.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

Subsidies, supply chain localisation, and technological sovereignty are reshaping the global allocation of capital.

Globalisation hasn't ended, but it's no longer organised solely around lowest cost. States are seeking supply security, control of key technologies, and domestic production capacity.

For companies, that means investment location becomes a strategic decision involving tax incentives, energy availability, regulatory regime, political risk, and access to talent.

The most successful groups won't simply relocate production. They'll design a multi-layered supplier network, reserve capacity, and contractual mechanisms enabling a quick change of source.

Project management must therefore link the commercial, legal, technological, and geopolitical layers into a single decision.

Industrial policy has re-emerged as an active tool across major economies, departing from decades of relatively hands-off approaches to strategic sectors.

Organisations operating across these regions increasingly need to track policy developments as a distinct risk category, since subsidies, tariffs, and localisation requirements can shift competitive dynamics with limited advance warning.

This new phase of industrial policy rewards organisations with the flexibility to adapt supply chains and investment plans as government priorities in each region continue to evolve.

Organisations that maintain dedicated policy-monitoring capability, rather than reacting only after major announcements, position themselves to adapt ahead of competitors still catching up.

Building flexibility into supply chain and investment planning provides a genuine hedge against policy shifts that remain difficult to predict with confidence.

Scenario planning that explicitly considers several plausible policy trajectories, rather than a single assumed outcome, better prepares an organisation for the genuine uncertainty this environment presents.
