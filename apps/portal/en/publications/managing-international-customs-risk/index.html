Managing International Customs Risk

A sudden change in customs policy can change the profitability of an entire export-import business line overnight.

Nermin Sefić31 July 2026

A sudden change in customs policy can change the profitability of an entire export-import business line overnight.

Companies engaged in international trade increasingly face rapid shifts in customs policy that can directly and significantly affect the profitability of individual product lines almost overnight.

Tracking the announcement and likelihood of trade-policy changes in key markets, rather than reacting only after they take effect, buys time to adjust pricing strategy or the supply chain.

Diversifying sourcing and sales markets across multiple customs jurisdictions reduces a business's sensitivity to a policy change in any single country or trade bloc.

Scenario planning the margin impact of different customs outcomes, done in advance, enables faster decisions on price or supply-chain adjustments once a change actually happens.

Pre-clearance programmes and trusted trader status, where available, reduce exposure to the unpredictable delays that standard customs processing can introduce into supply chains.

Building customs risk assessment into supplier and route selection, not treating it as a separate logistics function, produces more resilient international operations.

Building relationships with customs brokers experienced in specific trade lanes provides practical knowledge that general compliance training alone cannot replicate.

Organisations that develop this proactive customs risk capability internally, rather than relying entirely on external brokers, retain more control over route and supplier decisions as trade conditions evolve. This internal expertise also proves valuable when evaluating new markets where customs complexity might otherwise remain hidden until problems emerge.

This expertise proves its worth precisely when trade conditions shift unexpectedly.
