Digital Twins for Industrial Facilities

A digital twin of a facility delivers real operational value only once sensor data updates continuously, not as a one-off 3D model.

Nermin Sefić1 August 2026

A digital twin of a facility delivers real operational value only once sensor data updates continuously, not as a one-off 3D model.

A digital twin is not a static 3D depiction of a facility, but a dynamic model continuously updated with sensor data, enabling scenario simulation before real-world implementation.

Predictive maintenance based on a digital twin recognises equipment deviations before failure, reducing unplanned downtime that is significantly costlier than planned maintenance.

Investment in a digital twin is justified only once there's a clear operational use case — energy optimisation, predictive maintenance, or process-change simulation — not as technology for its own sake.

Integrating a digital twin with existing ERP and MES systems is often a bigger challenge than building the model itself, and deserves equal attention in project planning.

Duplicate coverage of digital twin technology across separate publications reflects its growing relevance across multiple facets of industrial operations, from maintenance planning to energy efficiency.

Facilities investing in this technology should prioritise data quality and sensor reliability over modelling sophistication, since even the most advanced twin provides limited value if built on inaccurate underlying data.

Cross-functional teams spanning operations, engineering, and IT tend to extract more value from digital twin deployments than initiatives led by a single department in isolation.

Standardising sensor data formats across facilities, where an organisation operates more than one site, allows digital twin insights to be compared meaningfully across locations.

Sharing digital twin insights across departments, not confining them to the team that built the model, multiplies the value extracted from the underlying investment.
