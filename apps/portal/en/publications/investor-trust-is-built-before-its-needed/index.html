Investor Trust Is Built Before It's Needed

Organisations investing in transparent market communication during calm periods have more room to manoeuvre when uncertainty arrives.

Nermin Sefić3 August 2026

Investor and business partner trust is rarely built at the moment it is most needed, but through consistent and verifiable communication during periods when the market is not asking particularly critical questions.

Organisations that regularly publish financial indicators and business information, regardless of whether results are especially favourable at that moment, build a reputation for reliability that is not easily lost at the first more challenging period.

When a period of uncertainty arrives, an organisation with a long history of transparent communication has significantly more room to manoeuvre than one that addresses the market only in moments of crisis.

GNK ASG d.o.o. continuously publishes publicly available financial indicators precisely with the aim of building a long-term, verifiable relationship of trust with the market.

Organisations that wait until they need investor goodwill to begin building it typically discover the relationship capital they need simply is not there.

The relationship capital built through years of consistent, honest communication becomes most valuable precisely during the moments an organisation can least afford to lack it.

This principle applies equally to smaller, privately held organisations seeking future capital as it does to those already publicly traded.

Organisations sometimes discover this principle only during a genuine crisis, when they realise the trust needed to weather difficult news simply was not cultivated during calmer periods. By then, rebuilding that foundation under pressure proves considerably harder than maintaining it consistently would have been.

This lesson tends to be learned expensively, if at all, only after the moment goodwill was most needed had already passed.
