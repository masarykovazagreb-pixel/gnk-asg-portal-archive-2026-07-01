Managing Flexible Work Models and Productivity

Measuring productivity by hours spent online instead of by actual output creates a conflict in hybrid models that doesn't have to exist.

Nermin Sefić31 July 2026

Measuring productivity by hours spent online instead of by actual output creates a conflict in hybrid models that doesn't have to exist.

The shift to hybrid and remote work models revealed that many organisations lacked clear performance indicators independent of physical presence, so they reached for activity tracking as a proxy instead.

Tracking hours logged into the system, instead of actual task completion and results achieved, creates a culture of visible busyness that doesn't reflect real productivity.

Defining clear, measurable goals per role, regardless of whether an employee works from the office or from home, allows for a fairer and more accurate assessment of contribution than presence tracking does.

Flexibility in the work model and clear accountability for results are not opposing goals — organisations that successfully combine both build trust, rather than surveillance, as the basis of the employee relationship.

Measuring output and outcomes rather than hours logged shifts management attention toward what flexible work models are actually meant to optimise for.

Regular check-ins focused on outcomes, rather than activity monitoring, build the trust flexible work models depend on to function well long-term.

This outcome-focused approach requires managers to develop new skills in setting clear expectations, since ambient office presence no longer substitutes for explicit communication.

Organisations that pair outcome-focused management with genuine trust in employee autonomy report stronger engagement than those that simply relocate traditional oversight mechanisms into a flexible work context. This cultural shift often requires more deliberate change management than the technical logistics of flexible work arrangements themselves.
