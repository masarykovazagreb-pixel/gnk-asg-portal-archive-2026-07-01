Third-Party Technology Vendor Audits

Assessing the security of your own systems is meaningless if it doesn't extend to suppliers who have access to the same data.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

Assessing the security of your own systems is meaningless if it doesn't extend to suppliers who have access to the same data.

Most serious security incidents in recent years didn't originate inside a company's primary system, but through a supplier or subcontractor with legitimate access.

A standard compliance questionnaire filled out once at contract signing doesn't reflect a supplier's actual security practice two or three years later.

A practical framework includes periodic review of supplier access rights, a clear clause obligating incident reporting within a defined deadline, and the right to independent verification for critical suppliers.

The cost of third-party audits is almost always lower than the cost of an incident that originated through a supplier outside the company's direct control.

Regular, scheduled vendor audits, not just audits triggered by a specific concern, catch gradual deterioration in vendor practices before it develops into a significant incident.

This regular cadence, even when no specific concern has been raised, catches the kind of gradual drift that only becomes visible over time.

GNK ASG d.o.o. maintains a scheduled vendor audit cadence across the GNK DINAMO Ltd. Group, independent of whether specific concerns have been raised.

Organisations that maintain genuine audit rigour across their full vendor portfolio, not concentrating scrutiny only on the largest or most visible relationships, catch emerging risks in smaller vendor relationships before they grow into significant exposures. This comprehensive approach requires resource allocation that many organisations initially resist, though the alternative of selective scrutiny leaves meaningful gaps in actual risk coverage.
