Managing Liquidity Crisis Scenarios

A liquidity crisis plan that hasn't been tested against a specific scenario usually exists only on paper.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

A liquidity crisis plan that hasn't been tested against a specific scenario usually exists only on paper.

A liquidity plan typically anticipates a single, moderately unfavourable scenario, while real crises rarely unfold according to just one script. The combination of a revenue drop, extended collection periods, and a simultaneous credit-line withdrawal creates pressure that isn't visible if each factor is tested separately.

Defining at least three scenarios — mild, severe, and a combined shock — with pre-agreed triggers for each (e.g., collections dropping below a certain threshold activating pre-prepared measures) turns crisis response from improvisation into executing a decision already made.

Pre-negotiated credit facilities, arranged before they are needed, provide considerably more reliable access to emergency liquidity than attempting to arrange financing during the crisis itself.

Regularly testing these pre-arranged facilities, not just establishing them once, confirms they remain genuinely accessible when actually needed.

Documenting the specific conditions under which each facility can be activated removes ambiguity that could otherwise slow response during an actual crisis.

Organisations that maintain relationships with multiple potential liquidity sources, not just a single primary facility, retain more genuine optionality when a crisis scenario actually unfolds. This diversification of liquidity sources mirrors the broader principle of avoiding concentration risk across other areas of treasury management.
