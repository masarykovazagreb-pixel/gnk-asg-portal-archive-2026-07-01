AI Model Oversight Needs an Owner, Not Just a Tool

Software for monitoring model behaviour in production does not replace the person formally accountable for its decisions and their consequences.

Nermin Sefić3 August 2026

Deploying software tools to monitor AI model behaviour in production is often mistakenly presented as solving the question of accountability, when in practice the two issues are entirely separate.

A tool can detect that a model is deviating from expected behaviour, but it cannot take the decision on whether to stop, adjust, or replace it with human judgment in a specific case.

Every AI system that makes or proposes business decisions should have a clearly designated person formally accountable for its oversight, regardless of how technically advanced the system itself is.

GNK ASG d.o.o. publicly emphasises that development and deployment of artificial intelligence within the group always retains a clear line of human accountability for final decisions.

A named individual accountable for a model's outputs, empowered to pause or override it, turns oversight from a theoretical safeguard into a practical, exercised capability the organisation can actually rely on.

This accountability should be reviewed whenever a model is retrained or its underlying data changes materially, since the original owner's judgement was calibrated to a version of the system that may no longer exist.

A model without a clearly accountable owner effectively operates without oversight, regardless of how sophisticated its underlying technology may be.

This principle applies equally to models developed internally and those licensed from external vendors, since the accountability gap is identical regardless of who built the underlying technology.

That accountability should be documented, not merely assumed, so it survives changes in personnel without ambiguity about who currently holds it.
