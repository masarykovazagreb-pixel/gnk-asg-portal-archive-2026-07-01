Securing Industrial Control Systems (OT)

Operational technology that controls physical processes requires a different security approach than standard IT systems, because an attack here has physical consequences.

Nermin Sefić31 July 2026

Operational technology (OT) that controls physical processes requires a different security approach than standard IT systems, because an attack here has physical consequences.

Systems that control physical industrial processes — production lines, energy plants — are increasingly connected to the IT network for analytics and remote monitoring, inheriting its security risks along with the benefits.

Unlike an IT security incident, which typically means data loss, a compromised industrial control system can have direct physical consequences for equipment, worker safety, and the environment.

Network segmentation that separates operational technology from the standard business IT network, with strict control of connection points, remains a critical but often under-implemented safeguard.

The security team responsible for OT systems needs to understand industrial processes, not just standard IT security, because measures acceptable in an office environment can jeopardize the safety of a production process.

Operational technology systems, historically isolated from broader networks, increasingly require the same security rigour as IT systems as convergence between the two continues.

Specialised security expertise in operational technology, distinct from standard IT security skills, is increasingly necessary as this convergence continues.

Organisations should invest in this specialised expertise proactively, rather than discovering the gap only after a security incident specific to operational technology occurs.

Organisations that invest in this specialised operational technology security expertise proactively, before convergence with IT systems creates urgent vulnerabilities, position themselves considerably better than those addressing the gap reactively after an incident. This specialised expertise increasingly commands premium value as the boundary between traditional IT and operational technology continues to blur.
