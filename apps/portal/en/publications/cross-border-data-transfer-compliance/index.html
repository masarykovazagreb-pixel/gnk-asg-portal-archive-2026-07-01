Cross-Border Data Transfer Compliance

Cross-border data transfer requires a legal basis that changes faster than the average company's internal documentation.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

Cross-border data transfer requires a legal basis that changes faster than the average company's internal documentation.

Companies operating in multiple countries routinely transfer personal and business data across borders, often without formally verifying whether the legal basis for that transfer is still valid.

Changes in a country's regulatory status can retroactively undermine transfer mechanisms that were previously fully compliant, without the company noticing until an audit is conducted.

A practical approach includes mapping all cross-border data flows, naming a person responsible for tracking regulatory changes, and contract clauses enabling quick adjustment of the transfer mechanism.

The cost of a one-time compliance review is almost always lower than the cost of a business disruption if a regulator later determines a data transfer lacked a valid legal basis.

Regulatory status affecting cross-border transfer mechanisms can change without an organisation's awareness, which is why assigning a named person to track relevant jurisdictional developments matters as much as the initial compliance assessment itself.

The cost of a periodic compliance review remains consistently lower than the cost of a business disruption triggered by a regulator determining, after the fact, that a transfer mechanism no longer had a valid legal basis.

This mapping exercise, once completed, becomes considerably easier to maintain than it was to build from scratch.

GNK ASG d.o.o. maintains this mapping as a living document across the GNK DINAMO Ltd. Group, updated whenever a new cross-border data flow is introduced.

This ongoing maintenance, though modest in isolated instances, compounds into significant risk reduction as an organisation's international footprint grows over time.
