Digital Identity and Business Network Trust

An automated process is only worth as much as the system's ability to know who acted, with what authority, and over which document.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

An automated process is only worth as much as the system's ability to know who acted, with what authority, and over which document.

Digitalization speeds up document exchange, but at the same time increases the importance of verifying identity and authority. Knowing which address sent a message isn't enough.

A reliable process must link the person or system, legal authority, document version, time of action, and proof that content wasn't altered after signing.

Digital identity isn't just a security function. It reduces verification time, prevents unauthorised decisions, and enables higher-quality automation.

Without verifiable identity, automation only transmits uncertainty faster.

This publication is an original analysis; the sources cited serve as reference documentation.

Verifying counterparty identity has grown more complex as business increasingly happens across digital channels without the in-person verification once taken for granted.

Investing in robust digital identity verification reduces exposure to increasingly sophisticated impersonation attempts targeting business relationships specifically.

This investment pays particular dividends in high-value transactions, where the cost of a successful impersonation fraud can be substantial.

This verification infrastructure benefits from periodic testing against increasingly sophisticated impersonation techniques, since static defences tend to lag behind evolving attack methods over time.

Multi-factor verification combining several independent signals reduces reliance on any single point of failure in the identity confirmation process.

Periodic reassessment of verification methods against emerging fraud techniques keeps this defence current rather than static.

Combining technological verification with human judgement, rather than relying entirely on automated systems, catches sophisticated attempts that purely algorithmic checks might miss.
