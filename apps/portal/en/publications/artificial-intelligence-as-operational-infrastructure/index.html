Artificial Intelligence as Operational Infrastructure

AI's value emerges only once models are linked to processes, accountability, quality data, and decision control.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

AI's value emerges only once models are linked to processes, accountability, quality data, and decision control.

Organisations often buy AI tools before defining the problem, the process owner, and the success criterion. That approach produces demonstrations, but not sustainable business value.

Operational AI requires reliable data, access control, a decision log, human review, and a clear procedure for when a model errs. Without that, speed turns into risk.

The best model isn't necessarily the largest model. The right choice depends on cost, latency, privacy, explainability, and the consequences of a wrong decision.

GNK ASG is developing a framework in which AI helps prepare, classify, and personalise content, while final accountability and approval remain human.

Treating AI systems with the same operational discipline as any other critical infrastructure — monitoring, redundancy, documented recovery procedures — reflects the reality that many organisations now depend on these systems for functions core to daily operations.

This shift in framing changes budget conversations as well, since infrastructure-grade systems justify infrastructure-grade investment in redundancy and monitoring that a mere productivity tool would not.

Recognising this dependency early allows an organisation to plan for redundancy before an outage forces the question.

Budget owners who still categorise AI spend as experimental or discretionary, rather than operational, risk underfunding the resilience these systems now genuinely require.

That reclassification often follows only after a disruptive outage, which is precisely the expensive way to learn the lesson.

Board-level visibility into this dependency, not just technical team awareness, ensures the resource commitment matches the actual operational stakes involved.
