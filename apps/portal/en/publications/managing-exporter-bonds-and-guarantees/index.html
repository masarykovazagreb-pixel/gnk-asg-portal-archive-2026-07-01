Managing Exporter Bonds and Guarantees

A bank guarantee ties up a company's credit line even when never activated, and that hidden cost is rarely tracked systematically.

Nermin Sefić1 August 2026

A bank guarantee ties up a company's credit line even when never activated, and that hidden cost is rarely tracked systematically.

A performance or advance-payment guarantee formally isn't a cost until activated, but ties up part of a company's credit line just as real debt would.

Companies simultaneously holding multiple active guarantees toward different clients often lose track of their total tied-up exposure to banks.

A centralised register of all issued guarantees, with expiry dates and activation conditions, enables an accurate assessment of remaining credit capacity at any moment.

Timely release of expired or no-longer-needed guarantees frees up credit space for new business without requiring additional negotiations with the bank.

Regular reconciliation between issued guarantees and actual contractual obligations catches guarantees that should have been released but were simply overlooked.

Building guarantee release into standard project closeout procedures ensures this step is not simply forgotten once a project concludes successfully.

Assigning explicit ownership for this closeout step, rather than leaving it to whoever happens to notice, ensures the process actually happens consistently.

This proactive release discipline, while operationally modest in any single instance, compounds meaningfully over time for organisations issuing guarantees regularly across many concurrent projects. The freed credit capacity often proves valuable precisely when new opportunities requiring fresh guarantees emerge.
