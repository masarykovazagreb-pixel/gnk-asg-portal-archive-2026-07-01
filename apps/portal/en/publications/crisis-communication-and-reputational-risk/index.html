Crisis Communication and Reputational Risk

The quality of crisis communication is measured by the speed, accuracy, and consistency of information, not just reaction speed.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

The quality of crisis communication is measured by the speed, accuracy, and consistency of information, not just reaction speed.

Reputational risk rarely arises from a single event. More often it arises from how that event is communicated. For GNK ASG d.o.o. and GNK DINAMO Ltd., that means crisis communication preparation must precede the crisis itself.

A practical model distinguishes confirmed facts from assumptions at the moment of first reaction. Blending those categories early in a crisis increases the risk of having to correct the record later.

Message consistency over time is just as important as the speed of the first reaction. Changing the narrative without clear explanation further erodes trust.

The greatest risk is a premature public statement before key facts are confirmed. That risk is reduced by a clear approval process before publication.

Prepared crisis communication turns reputational risk into a manageable process rather than an unpredictable outcome. This publication is part of the GNK ASG Intelligence Desk system and is informational in nature.

The first hour after an incident becomes public often determines the total scope of reputational damage, regardless of whether the underlying claim proves accurate, making a pre-agreed response protocol disproportionately valuable relative to its preparation cost.

Transparent, consistent communication throughout a crisis, even when the underlying incident reflects a genuine organisational failing, tends to preserve more long-term trust than a defensive or delayed response would.

Preparing this protocol before a crisis occurs, not during one, is what makes the difference between a fast, coherent response and a slow, fragmented one.

Running periodic drills against realistic incident scenarios reveals gaps in the protocol long before an actual crisis exposes them under far less forgiving circumstances.
