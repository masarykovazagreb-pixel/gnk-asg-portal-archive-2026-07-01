Managing Data Quality for AI Models

An AI model trained on unverified data repeats and amplifies every error and bias that already existed in that data.

Nermin Sefić1 August 2026

An AI model trained on unverified data repeats and amplifies every error and bias that already existed in that data.

The quality of an AI model's output depends directly on the quality of the data it was trained on — the model cannot correct a systematic error or bias present in the input data, only replicate it at scale.

A lack of clear documentation on the origin and processing of training data makes it harder to later diagnose why a model produces unexpected or incorrect results in production.

Systematically checking the representativeness of training data — whether it genuinely covers all relevant use scenarios — prevents situations where a model performs well in testing but poorly in real-world use.

Continuous monitoring of model performance after deployment, with clearly defined thresholds for retraining, keeps output quality intact as real-world data shifts over time.

Establishing data quality thresholds before model training begins, rather than discovering quality issues after deployment, prevents costly retraining cycles caused by foundational data problems.

Establishing data quality ownership as a distinct role, separate from model development itself, ensures quality receives dedicated attention rather than being treated as incidental.

This dedicated ownership should include authority to delay model deployment when data quality concerns remain unresolved, not merely advisory input.

This dedicated data quality function should have clear escalation authority, empowered to delay deployment when quality thresholds are not met, rather than serving purely as an advisory voice easily overridden under deadline pressure. Organisations that grant this authority see measurably fewer downstream model failures traceable to foundational data issues.
