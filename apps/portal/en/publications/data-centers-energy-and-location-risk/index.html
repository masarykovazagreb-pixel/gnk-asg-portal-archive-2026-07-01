Data Centers: Energy and Location Risk

Computing capacity can no longer be planned without a local energy strategy and an assessment of grid limitations.

2 August 2026

Computing capacity can no longer be planned without a local energy strategy and an assessment of grid limitations.

Digital infrastructure is often described as global, but its critical risks are highly local. Connection power, grid stability, cooling, and water determine whether a project can actually grow.

A location offering cheap energy today can become constrained tomorrow due to new connections, regulatory rules, or local community priorities.

A financial model must therefore include the cost of backup power, energy contracts, connection timelines, and a scenario of reduced available capacity.

An AI strategy without an energy and location strategy remains a presentation, not a feasible project.

This publication is an original analysis; the sources cited serve as reference documentation.

Political stability and long-term regulatory predictability in a chosen region often matter as much as current energy pricing when evaluating true long-term operational risk of a data centre location.

Organisations that model multiple future energy price scenarios before committing to a location avoid the common mistake of optimising purely for current conditions that may not hold for the facility's full operational life.

Diversifying data centre locations across regions with different risk profiles reduces the impact of any single location-specific disruption on overall operations.

This diversification strategy requires balancing resilience benefits against the added operational complexity of managing multiple, geographically distinct facilities.

Organisations that revisit this analysis periodically, rather than treating the original location decision as permanent, can identify when shifting conditions warrant reconsidering their footprint.

Long-term power purchase agreements negotiated alongside site selection can lock in more favourable terms than negotiating each separately after the location decision is finalised.
