ESG Reporting: From Form to Evidence

ESG reporting gains value only once indicators can be independently verified, not just formally stated.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

ESG reporting gains value only once indicators can be independently verified, not just formally stated.

ESG reporting is often reduced to formally filling out forms. The value of such reporting within the business system of GNK ASG d.o.o. and GNK DINAMO Ltd. comes from the ability to independently verify every stated indicator.

A practical approach distinguishes three levels: the metric being measured, the methodology used to measure it, and evidence that the measurement was carried out as stated. Without all three levels, a report remains declarative.

Comparability over time requires a consistent methodology. A change in methodology must be explicitly flagged to avoid misreading the trend.

The greatest reputational risk arises when a target is presented as an achieved result. Clearly separating those categories in the report text directly reduces that risk.

ESG reporting becomes credible only once it moves from form to a demonstrable process. This publication is part of the GNK ASG Intelligence Desk system and is informational in nature.

ESG reporting increasingly faces scrutiny over whether disclosed claims are backed by verifiable evidence, not merely well-crafted narrative language.

Building an evidentiary trail behind each ESG claim, comparable in rigour to financial reporting documentation, protects an organisation as external scrutiny of these disclosures continues to intensify.

This shift from narrative to evidence-based reporting requires investment in data collection systems many organisations have not historically maintained for non-financial metrics.

Independent verification of key ESG metrics, comparable to financial audit, provides a credibility signal that self-reported claims alone increasingly fail to deliver in a more sceptical reporting environment.
