Knowledge Management and Institutional Memory

Institutional memory reduces repeated mistakes and preserves key knowledge as people, systems, or responsibilities change.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

Institutional memory reduces repeated mistakes and preserves key knowledge as people, systems, or responsibilities change.

Knowledge that exists only in an individual's experience isn't an organisational asset until it can be transferred and verified.

A practical system links decisions, procedures, sources, and lessons learned to clear content ownership.

Knowledge management increases the speed of learning and reduces dependence on individual people.

Structured documentation practices, embedded into standard workflows rather than treated as a separate task, capture institutional memory that would otherwise walk out the door with departing employees.

GNK ASG d.o.o. treats documented institutional knowledge as a genuine organisational asset across the GNK DINAMO Ltd. Group, not merely administrative record-keeping.

This practice becomes increasingly valuable as organisations grow and the informal networks that once carried institutional knowledge naturally become less reliable.

This structured capture becomes especially valuable during periods of organisational change, when the informal networks that traditionally carried institutional knowledge are most likely to be disrupted. Organisations that treat documentation as a continuous practice, not a project completed once, maintain considerably more resilient institutional memory over time.

GNK ASG d.o.o. treats this as a continuous discipline across the GNK DINAMO Ltd. Group, not a one-time documentation project.

Consistent application over years, not occasional effort, ultimately determines whether institutional memory genuinely persists through organisational change.

This discipline compounds meaningfully across an organisation's full history, not just any single transition.

Organisations that treat this as core practice, not a side project, see the difference most clearly during transitions.
