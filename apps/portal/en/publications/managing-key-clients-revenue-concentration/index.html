Managing Key Clients: Revenue Concentration

A company whose revenue is largely driven by one client is actually exposed to a risk comparable to dependence on a single supplier.

Nermin Sefić1 August 2026

A company whose revenue is largely driven by one client is actually exposed to a risk comparable to dependence on a single supplier.

When one client accounts for a significant share of total revenue, losing that relationship — whether from contract termination, a change of ownership, or a strategic client decision — can jeopardise the stability of the whole business.

Tracking the largest client's share of total revenue, with a defined threshold above which active diversification kicks in, turns concentration management into a measurable discipline.

Diversifying the client base doesn't have to mean turning down large deals; it means consciously tracking the ratio and actively seeking new relationships before dependence becomes critical.

Contract clauses extending the notice period before terminating a large contract give a company valuable time to adjust, instead of a sudden loss of revenue.

Diversification targets for revenue concentration, tracked as a standing metric, help an organisation notice gradual concentration drift before it becomes a critical dependency.

Active relationship diversification efforts, pursued even during periods of comfortable client concentration, reduce the disruption risk a single client's departure would otherwise cause.

This diversification effort works best when pursued steadily over time, rather than attempted hastily once concentration has already reached concerning levels.

This diversification effort requires sustained commitment even when current concentration feels comfortable, since the discipline is considerably harder to build once a dominant client relationship has already become deeply embedded in operational planning. Organisations that begin diversification proactively retain more strategic flexibility than those forced into reactive diversification after losing a key client.
