GNK DINAMO before EUIPO: trademark filing chronology and opposition status

A document-based chronology of the EUIPO proceedings around the GNK DINAMO trademark — who filed and when, the status of opposition B 003264455, and the current procedural steps.

Nermin Sefić5 August 2026

What this is about

A proceeding is currently pending before the European Union Intellectual Property Office (EUIPO) concerning applications for the trademark "GNK DINAMO". This is a technical but publicly relevant matter — who requested EU-wide protection for that name, and when, and how those requests relate to one another. This overview presents the chronology as it appears in official documents filed with EUIPO, with a clear note that this is a proceeding still in progress, not a final decision.

Chronology per the opposition B 003264455 file

According to the procedural status summary filed by GNK DINAMO Ltd. in opposition B 003264455, the sequence of events is as follows:

  • 12 July 2025 — GNK DINAMO Ltd. files EU trademark (EUTM) application No. 019217073 for the word sign "GNK DINAMO", in Classes 25 (clothing), 35 (business services) and 41 (education/entertainment/sport).
  • 19 August 2025 — Građanski nogometni klub "Dinamo" (the football club) files opposition B 003245991 against that application, relying on earlier Croatian trademarks DINAMO (registrations Ž980668 and Z20081114). At that point, according to the filing, revocation proceedings against those trademarks had not yet been formally initiated.
  • 30 September 2025 — Revocation proceedings (for non-use) are initiated against those same Croatian trademarks, Ž980668 and Z20081114, before the competent national authority.
  • 13 November 2025 — The football club files its own new EUTM application No. 019275851, for the identical sign "GNK DINAMO" — 44 days after the revocation proceedings against its own trademarks had already begun.
  • 8 June 2026 — GNK DINAMO Ltd. files opposition B 003264455 against that later club application, relying on its own earlier July 2025 filing.
  • 9 July 2026 — EUIPO suspends the earlier opposition B 003245991, because the Croatian trademarks on which it relies are themselves at risk in revocation/invalidity proceedings.
  • 23 July 2026 — EUIPO confirms the procedural framework for B 003264455 and sets a deadline of 28 November 2026 for the submission of facts, evidence and arguments.

What GNK DINAMO Ltd. argues in its filing

In its submission, GNK DINAMO Ltd. — through authorised representative Nermin Sefić — argues that its application of 12 July 2025 has an earlier filing date than the club's November 2025 application, that both signs are identical ("GNK DINAMO"), and that they cover overlapping goods and services in Classes 25, 35 and 41. On that basis, it requests that the later club application be refused, or, in the alternative, that the proceeding be suspended until the status of the earlier application 019217073 is finally determined.

The submission specifically highlights a procedural circumstance: the club filed its new application only after revocation proceedings against its own, older Croatian trademarks were already underway — a fact which, according to the filing, the club, as proprietor of those rights, "knew or had to know".

Important — this is one side of an active proceeding

It is worth stating plainly: this is a submission from one party in a dispute that EUIPO has not yet resolved. The football club has an opportunity to respond to these arguments by a set deadline, and the final decision rests with the Office, not with either party. Nothing in this overview constitutes a ruling on who is "right" — it simply reports what has been formally, documentarily submitted, and the current procedural status.

Who currently leads the association "Dinamo"

For readers unfamiliar with this context: the association Građanski nogometni klub "Dinamo" is currently led by club president Zvonimir Boban, with Zvonimir Manenica and Hrvoje Puhalo serving on the management board. Manenica took over the finance and general affairs portfolio on the board from June 2025, while Puhalo is responsible for sales and marketing. This information comes from publicly available sources — the club's official website and media reporting on the appointments — and is included here strictly as context on who institutionally represents the association, not as a statement by any of them about this specific dispute, for which we have no source.

How the EU trademark protection system works

Understanding this specific case is easier with the general framework in mind. An EU trademark (EUTM, formerly known as a Community trademark) is a single right valid across all 27 EU member states simultaneously, unlike a national trademark valid in only one country. Applications are filed with the European Union Intellectual Property Office (EUIPO), headquartered in Alicante, Spain.

The system operates on a clear first-to-file principle — whoever first requests protection for a given sign, for given classes of goods and services, generally has priority over a later applicant for the same or a similar sign. That principle explains why the filing date — 12 July 2025 versus 13 November 2025 in this case — is such a significant fact in the chronology described above.

Once an application is published, third parties have a three-month window to file an opposition if they believe the new application infringes their earlier rights — whether an earlier EU trademark or an earlier national trademark recognised in any member state. That is precisely the mechanism through which the club, on 19 August 2025, challenged GNK DINAMO Ltd.'s application, relying on older Croatian trademarks.

Classes 25, 35 and 41 — what they actually cover

The Nice Classification, the international system EUIPO uses to categorise goods and services, divides all possible subjects of protection into 45 classes. The three classes named in this dispute are particularly relevant to the sporting context:

  • Class 25 covers clothing, footwear and headgear — in a sporting context, this includes jerseys, sportswear and fan merchandise.
  • Class 35 covers business services, including advertising, business management and, significantly, retail — which includes physical and online merchandise shops.
  • Class 41 covers education, entertainment and sporting activities — a class that directly covers the organisation of football competitions, sporting events and related services.

The overlap of interest in precisely these three classes is not coincidental — both parties, according to the publicly available applications, are seeking protection in the areas most closely tied to the identity and commercial value of the name "Dinamo" in a sporting context.

What "revocation for non-use" means

One of three parallel mechanisms active in this case is the revocation of a trademark for non-use — a legal tool available in nearly all trademark systems, including both the Croatian and EU systems. The principle is straightforward: a trademark exists to protect genuine market use of a sign, not to reserve a name indefinitely without any intention of using it. If a trademark owner does not use a registered sign on the market, for the goods or services it is registered for, for a continuous period of five years, any interested third party may request revocation of that trademark in those classes.

In the case described, the revocation proceedings initiated on 30 September 2025 concern Croatian trademarks Ž980668 and Z20081114, both registered under the name DINAMO. The outcome of those proceedings directly determines whether those older Croatian trademarks can even serve as a valid basis for opposition B 003245991, which the club filed against GNK DINAMO Ltd.'s application — which is why EUIPO decided, on 9 July 2026, to suspend that opposition pending resolution of the question.

The difference between EU and national proceedings

It is worth noting that this case involves proceedings running in parallel at two levels — before EUIPO (for the EU trademark) and before the Croatian State Intellectual Property Office, DZIV (for the Croatian national trademarks currently subject to revocation for non-use). These two proceedings are not formally merged, but they are substantively closely linked, because the validity of the Croatian trademarks directly affects whether they can serve as a basis for the EU-level opposition. It is precisely this interdependency — not any formal consolidation of proceedings — that explains why EUIPO explicitly tied its suspension decision to the status of the national proceedings.

Typical timeline for proceedings of this kind

EUIPO opposition proceedings on average take between twelve and eighteen months from filing to a first-instance decision, depending on case complexity and the number of deadline extensions the parties request during the so-called "cooling-off" period provided for amicable settlement. When, as in this case, one proceeding is formally suspended pending the outcome of another, related proceeding, the total time to a final resolution can extend to several years — because, in practice, the clock on the main proceeding stops while the precondition it depends on remains unresolved.

The deadline set for submitting further facts and arguments in opposition B 003264455 — 28 November 2026 — represents only one step in that longer process, not a final decision date.

Who the parties are — a brief profile

GNK DINAMO Ltd. is a company registered in Boulder, Colorado, USA, founded by Nermin Sefić. It operates in Zagreb through the foreign branch GNK DINAMO Ltd, Podružnica Zagreb, registered on 11 July 2025 at Aleja Matije Ljubeka 33. GNK ASG d.o.o., also under ownership connected to Sefić, is a Zagreb-based company whose stated business activities include technology and sports-analytics services.

Građanski nogometni klub "Dinamo" is a registered association headquartered at Maksimirska cesta 128, Zagreb, founded in 1911 as HŠK Građanski, and operating under the name Dinamo since 1945. The club carries a long sporting tradition, with numerous Croatian championship titles, and its Stadion Maksimir is home to the first team.

Why the name "Dinamo" is so sensitive

The name "Dinamo", in a Croatian but also wider regional context, carries significant symbolic weight tied to decades of sporting history, fan culture and local identity in the city of Zagreb. That symbolic value explains why the question of who may commercially use that name — on clothing, in marketing, or in organising sporting events — is not merely a technical legal matter, but touches on broader public perceptions of what the name "Dinamo" represents and who has a legitimate claim to speak in its name.

Precisely because of that sensitivity, EUIPO proceedings like the one described attract attention beyond legal specialists — because their outcome, whenever it becomes final, carries consequences beyond the narrow legal context of the trademark registration itself.

Next steps worth following

Beyond the 28 November 2026 deadline for submitting arguments in opposition B 003264455, readers wishing to follow the development of this case should watch three parallel tracks: first, the outcome of the revocation proceedings against Croatian trademarks Ž980668 and Z20081114 before the competent national authority; second, whether and when EUIPO resumes the suspended opposition B 003245991 once the status of those national trademarks is resolved; and third, EUIPO's final decision on opposition B 003264455 itself, the subject of this overview.

As none of these three tracks has yet concluded, any final assessment of the dispute's outcome at this point would be premature — which is precisely why this overview remains strictly limited to what is documented and procedurally confirmed, without speculation about the outcome.
