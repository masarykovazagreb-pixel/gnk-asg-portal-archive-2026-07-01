Measuring Digitalization Return on Investment

Digitalization without a defined success metric easily becomes a cost justified by trend, not by result.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

Digitalization without a defined success metric easily becomes a cost justified by trend, not by result.

The decision to digitalize a process is often made based on expectations, but rarely subsequently measured against predefined, concrete success indicators.

Reduced processing time, error counts before and after, or actual labour-hour savings are more measurable indicators than a general claim that a process is now 'digitalized'.

Comparing the real implementation cost — including the team's hidden adjustment time — against the measured saving gives a more realistic picture than the initial projection made before the project began.

The practice of a post-implementation review, conducted three to six months after rollout, reveals whether expected benefits were actually realised or the project just changed the shape of the same problem.

Defining success metrics before a digitalisation initiative begins, rather than retrospectively justifying the investment, produces more honest assessment of what actually delivered value.

Post-implementation review against these predefined metrics, conducted honestly even when results disappoint, produces more useful organisational learning than retrospective justification would.

This honest assessment, even when uncomfortable, produces genuine organisational learning that shapes better decisions about future digitalisation investment.

Organisations that maintain this measurement discipline across multiple digitalisation initiatives build comparative insight into which types of investment consistently deliver value, informing more effective prioritisation of future technology spending. This accumulated organisational learning proves increasingly valuable as digitalisation investment continues to compete for limited capital.

This honesty, even when uncomfortable, produces genuinely better future investment decisions.

This accumulated learning across multiple initiatives sharpens judgement for every future technology investment decision.
