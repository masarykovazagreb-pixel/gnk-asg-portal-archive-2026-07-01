Managing Supplier Quality Through Systematic KPI Tracking

A supplier's quality rarely collapses overnight — the decline is visible in the data long before it reaches the end customer.

Nermin Sefić31 July 2026

A supplier's quality rarely collapses overnight — the pattern of gradual decline is visible in the data long before it becomes a visible problem for the end customer.

Delivery quality from a given supplier rarely worsens suddenly — a gradual decline pattern, visible through indicators like on-time delivery rate and claim rate, precedes any problem visible to the end customer.

Systematically tracking key performance indicators (KPIs) for every critical supplier, rather than relying on a subjective sense of the relationship, allows for early intervention before problems escalate.

A regular quarterly review of results with the supplier, grounded in agreed, measurable indicators, turns relationship management from reactive firefighting into proactive collaboration.

Clearly defined thresholds that trigger a formal improvement plan or a search for alternatives prevent a declining supplier relationship from dragging on longer than is justified.

Sharing KPI performance directly with suppliers, not just tracking it internally, creates a shared basis for improvement conversations rather than one-sided compliance demands.

Regular review meetings built around shared KPI data create structured opportunities for improvement conversations that ad hoc communication rarely produces.

Selecting KPIs that genuinely reflect what matters most to the relationship, rather than metrics that are simply easiest to measure, produces more meaningful tracking.

Organisations that involve suppliers directly in defining relevant KPIs, rather than imposing metrics unilaterally, often achieve better buy-in and more genuine improvement than purely top-down quality mandates. This collaborative approach also surfaces operational constraints suppliers face that purely internal metric-setting might overlook.
