Managing Knowledge Transfer Across Generational Change

Home›Publications›Managing Knowledge Transfer Across Generational Change

The retirement of a generation of experienced employees often carries a loss of knowledge that was never formally documented.

1 August 2026

The retirement of a generation of experienced employees often carries a loss of knowledge that was never formally documented.

Experienced employees nearing retirement often carry knowledge of informal workarounds, historical decisions, and process quirks that was never formally documented because it always felt sufficient that 'that person just knows.'

Mentoring programs that systematically pair more experienced employees with younger colleagues before a planned departure transfer practical knowledge in a way formal documentation alone cannot replace.

Structured exit interviews with employees before retirement, focused on surfacing informal processes and the historical context behind decisions, are a common but underused tool for capturing institutional knowledge.

Organisations that systematically plan knowledge transfer ahead of generational change avoid a situation where a routine process suddenly becomes a mystery for the new team.

Structured mentorship programmes that pair departing expertise with incoming staff well before a transition, rather than during a rushed handover period, preserve considerably more institutional knowledge.

This structured approach works best when begun years, not months, before an anticipated transition, giving genuine time for knowledge to transfer naturally.

Documenting not just procedures but the reasoning behind them helps successors understand context that pure process documentation often fails to capture.

This extended timeline for knowledge transfer also allows successors to develop genuine judgement, not just procedural familiarity, through observed decision-making across a range of situations rather than compressed handover sessions. Organisations that under-invest in this transition timeline often discover the gap only once the departing expert is no longer available to consult.
