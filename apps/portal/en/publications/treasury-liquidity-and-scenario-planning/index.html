Treasury Liquidity and Scenario Planning

Having enough cash today doesn't mean having enough cash when obligations and investment requirements actually fall due.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

Having enough cash today doesn't mean having enough cash when obligations and investment requirements actually fall due.

Liquidity is a time structure, not a single number. An organisation can have significant funds but the wrong maturity schedule, currency, or usage restriction.

Treasury must link operating flows, tax obligations, debt, capital projects, and the minimum reserve needed for a disruption.

A scenario should show how long the group can keep operating if customers pay late, financing becomes more expensive, or certain revenue temporarily disappears.

The most valuable liquidity decision is often the one made before cash is urgently needed.

This publication is an original analysis; the sources cited serve as reference documentation.

Stress-testing liquidity positions against several distinct adverse scenarios reveals vulnerabilities that planning around a single expected case would not expose.

Regular refresh of these scenarios, incorporating lessons from actual market events as they occur, keeps stress testing relevant rather than academic.

This iterative refinement, informed by genuine market experience rather than purely theoretical scenarios, produces stress testing that remains grounded in realistic possibility.

Organisations that treat scenario planning as a living practice, continuously refined as market conditions and organisational circumstances evolve, maintain stress testing that remains genuinely relevant rather than gradually becoming an outdated academic exercise. This ongoing refinement requires treasury teams to actively incorporate lessons from actual market developments, not simply revisit static scenarios on a fixed schedule.

This refinement keeps stress testing grounded in genuine, evolving market reality.

Grounding these scenarios in genuine market experience keeps stress testing meaningfully realistic.
