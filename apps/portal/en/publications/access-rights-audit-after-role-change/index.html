Access Rights Audit After a Role Change

Access rights that outlive an employee's role change become the most common, and least frequently detected, security gap.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

Access rights that outlive an employee's role change become the most common, and least frequently detected, security gap.

When an employee changes department or leaves the company, removing access to old systems is often delayed or forgotten entirely, leaving open access no one is actively monitoring.

The accumulation of outdated access rights over the years creates a security risk proportional to the number of role changes in the organisation, not just the number of current employees.

Automated access-rights review tied to the HR system — which flags the need for reassessment at every role change — closes that gap without relying on an administrator's manual memory.

A quarterly review of all active access, with mandatory manager confirmation that the permissions are still needed, is standard practice in mature security programs.

Automating this review through integration with the HR system, rather than relying on periodic manual checks, closes the gap for organisations where role changes happen frequently across large teams.

The gap between formal policy and actual practice tends to widen precisely in organisations that assume a well-written policy is sufficient on its own, without the operational mechanism to enforce it consistently.

GNK ASG d.o.o. treats this review as a standing operational requirement across the GNK DINAMO Ltd. Group, not an annual formality revisited only when an audit approaches.

Building that review into the standard offboarding and role-transition checklist ensures it happens consistently, rather than depending on any single person remembering to initiate it each time.
