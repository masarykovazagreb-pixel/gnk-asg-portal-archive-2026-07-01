Digital Signatures: Legal Validity of Documents

Not all forms of electronic signing carry the same level of legal certainty, and the difference only becomes visible in a dispute.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

Not all forms of electronic signing carry the same level of legal certainty, and the difference only becomes visible in a dispute.

A scanned signature inserted into a PDF and a qualified electronic signature under the eIDAS regulation are legally different categories, even though in everyday practice they look similar.

For higher-value contracts or cross-border transactions, the level of legal certainty of a digital signature should be a deliberate decision, not the default setting of whatever software happens to be in use.

The documentation trail — who signed, when, from which device, and with what identity-verification method — matters just as much as the signature itself when a document needs to serve as evidence.

Companies that standardise the level of electronic signature according to the value and type of document avoid having its validity questioned later, at the moment it's most costly.

Legal recognition of digital signatures varies meaningfully across jurisdictions, making it essential to confirm validity requirements before relying on them for cross-border agreements.

Maintaining clear audit trails alongside digital signatures, documenting the full signing process, strengthens their evidentiary value if an agreement is later disputed.

Organisations operating across multiple jurisdictions benefit from standardising on signature solutions that meet the strictest applicable legal standard, rather than varying approaches by market.

Reviewing signature solution compliance annually, as regulations evolve across different jurisdictions, prevents an organisation from relying on validity assumptions that may no longer hold.

Retaining signed documents alongside their full audit trail for the duration required by each relevant jurisdiction avoids gaps in evidentiary support years after signing.
