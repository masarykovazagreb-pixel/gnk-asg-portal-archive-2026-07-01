Semiconductor Supply Resilience: Diversification

Dependence on a single semiconductor component manufacturer exposes a company to risk beyond its own control over production.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

Dependence on a single semiconductor component manufacturer exposes a company to risk beyond its own control over production.

Global concentration of advanced semiconductor component manufacturing in a small number of geographic locations creates systemic risk that an individual company can't eliminate, only manage.

Geopolitical tensions, natural disasters, or a simple surge in demand can simultaneously hit all customers dependent on the same manufacturer, regardless of individual contract size.

Qualifying alternative suppliers for critical components, even at a higher unit price, acts as insurance against a complete production halt at the moment of a supply disruption.

Strategic stockpiles of critical components, though they tie up working capital, can be a justified cost compared to the price of a complete production stoppage due to unavailability of one component.

Qualifying alternative suppliers before a disruption occurs, rather than during one, preserves options that emergency sourcing under time pressure would not allow.

Maintaining ongoing relationships with qualified alternative suppliers, even without immediate need, preserves the option value that becomes critical during a genuine shortage.

This ongoing relationship maintenance, though requiring modest continuous investment, proves considerably less costly than attempting to qualify entirely new suppliers under genuine time pressure.

Organisations that treat supplier qualification as an ongoing capability, not a one-time project completed and then set aside, maintain genuine optionality that proves invaluable during the periodic supply disruptions this sector has repeatedly experienced. This ongoing investment, though carrying real cost even absent immediate need, functions as a form of insurance against disruptions that are difficult to predict but consistently recurring.
