Scenario Planning for Energy Costs

Energy costs should be viewed through multiple scenarios of price, consumption, and availability, not a single projection.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

Energy costs should be viewed through multiple scenarios of price, consumption, and availability, not a single projection.

A single forecast hides the range of possible outcomes and creates a false sense of precision.

Scenarios should link price, consumption, contractual obligations, and the ability to adjust operations.

The value of scenarios isn't in predicting the future but in decisions prepared in advance for different conditions.

Modelling several distinct energy price trajectories, rather than a single central forecast, prepares an organisation for a wider range of plausible future outcomes.

This modelling exercise benefits from periodic refresh as energy market conditions evolve, rather than being treated as a one-time analysis.

GNK ASG d.o.o. incorporates this refreshed modelling into its regular treasury planning cycle across the GNK DINAMO Ltd. Group.

Organisations that build genuine flexibility into their operational planning, informed by this scenario modelling, can respond more nimbly to energy price shifts than those committed to rigid assumptions based on a single anticipated trajectory. This flexibility often proves more valuable than attempting to predict energy prices with precision, which remains genuinely difficult even for specialist forecasters.
