Managing Cloud Costs Through FinOps Practice

A cloud bill that surprises the finance team every month usually means no one is tracking spend in real time.

Nermin Sefić1 August 2026

A cloud services bill that surprises the finance team every month usually means no one is systematically tracking spend in real time.

The elasticity of cloud infrastructure, its greatest advantage, is at the same time its biggest cost risk — resources are easy to spin up, and just as easy to forget running after they're no longer needed.

FinOps practice connects engineering teams, who decide on infrastructure, with the finance team, who tracks the cost, through shared accountability instead of separate reports that only meet at month's end.

Automated tagging of every resource by project and owner enables precise cost tracking down to the team level, not just the total bill for the whole organisation.

A regular review of unused or oversized resources, with clear accountability for shutting them down, prevents an accumulation of cost that becomes hard to justify after the fact.

Embedding cost visibility directly into engineering workflows, rather than isolating it within finance, helps the teams provisioning resources make more cost-aware decisions in real time.

Cross-functional FinOps teams, spanning finance and engineering, consistently produce more sustainable cost discipline than either function operating independently.

Regular cost reviews built into existing engineering rituals, rather than separate finance-driven audits, integrate cost awareness into daily decision-making more naturally.

Organisations that establish clear cost accountability at the team level, rather than centralising all cost decisions within finance, tend to see more sustainable long-term discipline emerge naturally. This distributed ownership model also scales more effectively as cloud usage grows across an increasingly complex organisation.
