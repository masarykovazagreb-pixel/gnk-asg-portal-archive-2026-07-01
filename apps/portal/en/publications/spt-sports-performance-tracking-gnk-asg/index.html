GNK ASG unveils SPT: athlete tracking with GPS, UWB and artificial intelligence

SPT (Sports Performance Tracking) connects a wearable sensor, watch, chest strap and smart scale with a platform that calculates load, readiness and injury risk in real time. Its software is already bought and built in by other manufacturers of similar systems — sometimes under their brand, but always with our tools, software and servers.

AuthorNermin SefićAuthor profileGNK ASG · GNK DINAMO Ltd. USA Group

In modern sport, results are increasingly decided before kick-off: by who trained too hard on Monday, who had a bad night on Thursday and whose sprints became slower than usual in the second half. SPT — Sports Performance Tracking is a system that captures these signals as they happen and turns them into decisions the staff can make in time: before the injury, not after it.

SPT at a glance

  • 20 Hz GNSS outdoors — GPS, Galileo, GLONASS and BeiDou at once
  • ±10 cm UWB indoors, where satellite signals cannot reach
  • IMU up to 1000 Hz, nine axes, IP67 housing
  • < 1 second from sensor to bench, even when the internet drops
  • 75 sports in the database · app in 10 languages · presentation in 24 languages
  • Roles: coach, strength & conditioning coach, athlete, scout and administrator
  • 64 measured parameters — movement, mechanical load, heart, sleep, body, nutrition, hydration, tests, wellness and tactics (list)

From sensor to bench in under a second

The heart of the system is the SPT Pod, a sensor worn in a compression-vest pocket between the shoulder blades. The spot is no accident: while running it is the stillest point of the body with the clearest view of the sky, so the satellite track and the acceleration data are cleaner than on the wrist or the hips.

The Pod receives four satellite systems 20 times per second, while a nine-axis inertial sensor records accelerations, rotations and impacts up to 1000 times per second. When play moves indoors, where satellite signals cannot reach, the sensor switches to UWB on its own: ceiling anchors locate the athlete to within about ten centimetres. At the stadium, the SPT Edge One server receives the data, calculates metrics locally and sends them to the bench in under a second — even when the internet fails.

Showcase: one week at an American club

To show how this works in practice, we use a fictional but realistic example: Coastline United SC, a professional football club from California, and a staff of around thirty people working from Monday to match day with a single source of truth. The club and the data are simulated — the features are real.

  • Monday (MD+2) — recovery. Morning readiness from HRV, sleep and resting heart rate sorts players into green, amber or red. Red players get an individual plan.
  • Tuesday and Wednesday (MD-4, MD-3) — the heaviest load of the week. The S&C coach tracks high-speed running, sprints and the number of hard accelerations and decelerations against the target dose for each position.
  • Thursday (MD-2) — tactics and speed, lower volume. SPT flags players whose acute-to-chronic workload ratio is climbing too fast.
  • Friday (MD-1) — activation. The line-up is decided with data, not gut feeling alone.
  • Match day. Live on the bench, on the sideline tablet and on the big screen in the control room; the report builds itself.

In the 67th minute of the demo match against Bay City, number 9 sprints at 32.2 km/h and scores to make it 3–1. At the same moment a red marker lights up next to number 6: his acute-to-chronic workload ratio has reached 1.52, above the threshold the staff set themselves. The system does not make the decision — it puts it in front of the coach in time, with the reason and a substitution suggestion.

Key features: what they do and why

SPT has dozens of modules, from nutrition to scouting. These are the ones that change staff decisions the most:

FeatureWhat it doesWhy it matters
ACWR — acute:chronic workload ratioCompares the load of the last 7 days with the average of the last 28 days (exponentially weighted averages).Sudden spikes in load are associated with a higher risk of soft-tissue injury. A zone of roughly 0.8 to 1.3 is considered balanced; values above 1.5 are considered risky.
High-speed running and sprintsCounts metres and repetitions above speed thresholds tailored to sport and position (in football often around 20 and 25 km/h).Hamstrings and groin are most sensitive to maximal-speed exposure — too little is as dangerous as too much.
Accelerations and decelerationsCounts sharp changes of speed, e.g. above 3 m/s², from the 1000 Hz inertial sensor.Braking is the most mechanically demanding load for muscles and tendons, and GPS alone does not see it well enough.
ReadinessCombines HRV, resting heart rate, sleep and a short questionnaire into a morning score: green, amber or red.The same session is not the same stimulus for a rested and an exhausted athlete.
Monotony and strainMeasures how similar the days of the week are and how large the week's total stress is.Uniformly high load without recovery days often precedes illness and overtraining.
Live control roomUp to 12 tiles: map, heart rate, speeds, alarms or a half-pitch comparison of two players.The analyst and the coach see the same picture in the same second.
Body compositionThe bioimpedance scale measures weight, muscle, fat, water, visceral fat and basal metabolism.Nutrition and load are planned on the body's real condition, not on impressions.
Reports and printingPost-session and post-match reports, the athlete file and print-outs for the board.Less paperwork, more time for players.

An important note: SPT provides performance and wellbeing indicators, not a medical diagnosis. Thresholds are set per club, sport and athlete, and decisions remain with people — the coach, the S&C coach and the doctor.

From a metre of running to a gram of protein: SPT measures movement, mechanical load, heart, sleep, body, nutrition, hydration, environment, tests, wellness and tactics. Every number has a purpose — each one comes with what it shows and why it matters.

What the analyses show

  • Morning readiness — Who can go full intensity today and who needs an adjusted session: a green/amber/red traffic light for the whole squad before the first session.
  • Session report — Whether the session hit its goal: planned versus delivered load per player and position, with deviations highlighted.
  • Weekly risk — Who is entering the risk zone: ACWR, monotony, asymmetry and the AI estimate, with reasons and a recommendation for next week.
  • Nutrition versus load — Whether intake matched expenditure: energy availability, protein and carbohydrate by microcycle day, with a meal and hydration plan.
  • Benchmarking and development — How a player compares with their own norm, position and league profile; the season trend for scouting and youth development.
  • Return after injury — When a return is safe: the RTP index compares current values with pre-injury values, step by step.

Thresholds (e.g. HSR > 19.8 km/h, ACWR 0.8–1.3) are reference values from sports science; in SPT they are tuned per club, sport, age and athlete. SPT provides performance and wellbeing indicators, not a medical diagnosis.

SPT inside other manufacturers' devices

It is less well known that SPT does not run only on our own devices. SPT software is also bought and built into their products by other manufacturers of similar athlete-tracking systems. Sometimes it runs under their brand — but always with our tools, our software and on our servers.

  • Firmware SDK and protocol — GNSS/IMU fusion, UWB ranging, BLE sync and OTA updates.
  • Cloud and edge servers — real-time ingestion, processing and storage on SPT infrastructure or the partner's.
  • White-label app — the partner's logo, colours and domain; our code and our updates.
  • Analytics engine and AI as an API — ACWR, readiness, injury risk, biomechanics and reports.
  • Open API and export — JSON and CSV to video analysis, medical systems and the club ERP.

We do not publish partner names: on request we sign an NDA and work under the partner's brand. What they all have in common — every new version we develop for SPT also reaches their users.

Continuous development

SPT is not a finished product but a platform that grows with clubs and partners. A new version ships every few weeks. The latest, SPT 2.5, added AI-assisted risk assessment, scouting with percentiles by position and age, a medical and rehabilitation module, league comparison, biomechanics, a meal planner, cycle tracking and an open API. The Windows 11 desktop version installs with one click, and the system also runs as a web app.

From football to rowing: 75 sports

Football is our showcase, but SPT is built for 75 sports. Each has its own positions, metrics and norms: from basketball, handball, volleyball and futsal indoors with UWB, through rowing, water polo and swimming, to athletics, cycling, skiing and motorsport — car racing, motorcycling and karting.

For amateur athletes, a watch and the app are enough: heart rate, sleep, recovery and a training plan that adapts to the day. For academies and young athletes aged 12 and over there are age curves, growth and load monitoring, and parental consent.

The data belongs to the club and the athlete

Each role sees only what it needs: coach, S&C coach, athlete, scout and administrator have different permissions. Clubs are stored in the system under codes, minors' data is additionally protected and injuries are entered by authorised staff. The data stays with the club even when people leave.

Conclusion

SPT brings together what sport kept apart for a long time: sensors, training science and the staff's daily work. The result is not another spreadsheet but a timely decision — who trains at full intensity today, who needs rest and who is ready for the match.
