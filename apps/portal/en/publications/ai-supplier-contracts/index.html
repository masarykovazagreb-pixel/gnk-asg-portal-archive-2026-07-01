AI Supplier Contracts

A standard software contract often doesn't cover the risks of a model, data, and variable AI results.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

A standard software contract often doesn't cover the risks of a model, data, and variable AI results.

An AI service isn't just software. It includes a model that can change, data that can be used to improve the service, and a result whose accuracy depends on context.

The contract must clearly state whether the supplier may use input data for training, where data is processed, how an incident is reported, and what happens after the service ends.

A minimum level of documentation, notice of a material model change, and a way to verify a result affecting rights, finances, or contractual obligations must be defined.

An exit strategy must exist before entering the service.

This publication is an original analysis; the sources cited serve as reference documentation.

Clauses addressing data ownership, model retraining rights, and liability for erroneous outputs deserve the same scrutiny as pricing terms, since these provisions often determine the real cost of an AI vendor relationship years after signing.

Organisations that negotiate these terms before signing, rather than after a dispute arises, retain considerably more leverage than those attempting to renegotiate an established vendor relationship.

These clauses deserve revisiting periodically as the vendor relationship evolves, not just at the point of initial signature.

Legal teams unfamiliar with the specific risks AI vendor relationships introduce may default to standard software contract templates that leave these critical gaps entirely unaddressed.

Reviewing these terms with input from technical staff, not legal alone, helps catch gaps that a purely contractual reading might miss.
