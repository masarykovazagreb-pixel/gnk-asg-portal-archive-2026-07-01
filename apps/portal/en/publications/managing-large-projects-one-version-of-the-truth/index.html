Managing Large Projects: One Version of the Truth

Project decisions break down when finance, legal, technology, and operations work from different data.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

Project decisions break down when finance, legal, technology, and operations work from different data.

A major project doesn't fail only from poor execution. It fails when teams understand the deadline, budget, scope, or acceptance criteria differently.

One version of the truth means every key data point has a source, an owner, a date, a status, and a change history. A meeting then serves decision-making, not arguing over which number is correct.

Every project must have an accountable person, an operational coordinator, legal and financial oversight, and a clear escalation procedure.

Worker output should be organised around deliverables and evidence, not the volume of activity.

A single, authoritative source of project status, accessible to all stakeholders, eliminates the confusion that arises when different teams work from inconsistent, independently maintained tracking documents.

Regular reconciliation between the authoritative source and any local tracking tools teams may still maintain catches drift before it causes genuine confusion.

This single-source discipline requires genuine organisational commitment, since the temptation to maintain convenient local copies persists even after a central system exists.

Organisations that invest in genuinely accessible, well-maintained central tracking systems see this discipline take hold more naturally than those relying purely on policy mandates without adequate underlying tooling. The system's usability directly determines whether teams embrace it or continue maintaining parallel local records out of habit or convenience.

Usability, more than policy alone, ultimately determines whether this discipline genuinely takes hold.

Well-maintained, genuinely usable systems earn adoption; mandated ones without adequate usability rarely do.
