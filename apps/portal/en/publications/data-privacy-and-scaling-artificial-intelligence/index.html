Data Privacy and Scaling Artificial Intelligence

Home›Publications›Data Privacy and Scaling Artificial Intelligence

AI systems can scale only if data sources, legal basis, and access controls are arranged in advance.

2 August 2026

AI systems can scale only if data sources, legal basis, and access controls are arranged in advance.

An AI system increases the value of data, but also increases the consequences of incorrect access, retention, or reuse of information.

Before scaling, it must be established which data a model may receive, where it's processed, how long it's kept, and who may see the result.

Privacy must not be a final check after development. It must be part of the architecture, testing, and supplier contracts.

A controlled system may be slower in its first phase, but is significantly faster once real deployment arrives.

As AI systems scale to process ever larger volumes of personal data, privacy controls that worked adequately at smaller scale often prove insufficient without deliberate redesign.

Building privacy considerations into system architecture from the start, rather than retrofitting them after scaling begins, avoids the far more disruptive process of redesigning data flows once the system is already in production.

GNK ASG d.o.o. treats privacy-by-design as a baseline requirement for any AI system deployed across the GNK DINAMO Ltd. Group, regardless of the scale at which that system initially operates.

Regular privacy impact assessments, conducted as the system scales rather than only at initial deployment, catch emerging risks that were not present or relevant at smaller operational scale.

Organisations that document data minimisation decisions as systems scale build a defensible record useful when regulators or auditors later ask why particular data categories were retained.
