Water Supply Resilience for Industrial Facilities

Industrial facilities dependent on a single water source carry an operational risk that climate change is making increasingly likely.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

Industrial facilities dependent on a single water source carry an operational risk that climate change is making increasingly likely.

Industrial processes requiring large volumes of water for cooling or production are increasingly facing the risk of temporary supply restrictions due to droughts and climate change in the regions where plants operate.

A water-risk assessment specific to the facility's location, not a generic country-level assessment, reveals the real exposure, which varies significantly between regions within the same country.

Recycling process water and investing in alternative sources, such as treated wastewater, reduce dependence on a single source and act as insurance against seasonal restrictions.

Dialogue with local authorities about long-term water availability for industrial use, before a restriction becomes urgent, gives a company time to adapt instead of reacting under pressure.

Facilities in water-stressed regions benefit from contingency planning that goes beyond regulatory minimums, given the operational disruption a genuine water shortage could cause.

Diversified water sourcing, where geographically and technically feasible, reduces dependency on any single source that could be disrupted by regional conditions.

This planning should be revisited periodically as regional water conditions evolve, rather than treated as a static assessment made once and never revisited.

Organisations operating in regions facing genuine water stress benefit from treating this resilience planning as an ongoing strategic priority, not a one-time assessment conducted and then set aside. This sustained attention becomes increasingly important as water availability patterns continue to shift in ways that historical planning assumptions may no longer adequately capture.
