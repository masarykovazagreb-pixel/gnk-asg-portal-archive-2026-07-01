Payment Infrastructure Resilience

A company dependent on a single payment channel carries an operational risk comparable to dependence on a single electricity supplier.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

A company dependent on a single payment channel carries an operational risk comparable to dependence on a single electricity supplier.

An outage at one bank, payment processor, or clearing system, however brief, directly halts a company's ability to pay suppliers or receive customer payments.

Diversifying payment channels — multiple banking relationships, alternative processors for critical transactions — reduces that exposure in a way similar to raw-material supplier diversification.

Testing scenarios of temporary unavailability of the primary payment channel, including a clear plan for who approves an alternative payment path, is rarely part of a standard business continuity plan.

The cost of maintaining an alternative payment channel is almost always lower than the cost of several days of operational downtime when the primary channel is unavailable.

Redundant payment processing pathways, tested regularly rather than assumed to function, prevent a single point of failure from halting an organisation's ability to transact.

Regular testing of failover procedures, not just their existence on paper, confirms an organisation can actually execute the transition when a primary pathway fails.

This regular testing discipline, though requiring dedicated time and resources, provides confidence that cannot be achieved through documentation review alone.

Organisations that maintain and genuinely test multiple payment pathways, rather than assuming redundancy exists without verification, discover configuration gaps and dependencies that would otherwise surface only during an actual outage. This testing discipline, while requiring dedicated effort, provides considerably more confidence than architectural design alone can offer.

This confidence cannot be achieved through architectural design or documentation review alone.
