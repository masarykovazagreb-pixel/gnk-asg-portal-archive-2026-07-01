Digital Twin for Industrial Facilities

A digital twin of a facility delivers real operational value only once sensor data updates continuously, not as a one-time 3D model.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

A digital twin of a facility delivers real operational value only once sensor data updates continuously, not as a one-time 3D model.

A digital twin isn't a static 3D depiction of a facility, but a dynamic model continuously updated with sensor data, enabling scenario simulation before real-world implementation.

Predictive maintenance based on a digital twin detects equipment deviations before failure, reducing unplanned downtime that is significantly costlier than scheduled maintenance.

Investment in a digital twin is justified only when there's a clear operational use case — energy optimisation, predictive maintenance, or process-change simulation — not as technology for its own sake.

Integrating a digital twin with existing ERP and MES systems is often a bigger challenge than building the model itself, and deserves equal attention in project planning.

A digital twin's value depends entirely on how accurately and how frequently it reflects the actual state of the physical facility it represents.

Investing in robust sensor infrastructure to keep the twin synchronised with reality matters as much as the modelling software itself, since a poorly synchronised twin can mislead more than it informs.

Facilities that update their digital twin only periodically, rather than continuously, sacrifice much of the real-time decision support this technology is meant to provide.

Facilities that treat the digital twin as a living system requiring ongoing investment, not a one-time project, extract considerably more operational value from the technology over its useful life.

Cross-training operational staff to interpret and act on digital twin outputs extends the technology's value beyond the specialist teams who initially deployed it.
