Cyber Resilience: Board Governance

The board must understand dependencies, recovery priorities, and the financial consequences of a business disruption.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

The board must understand dependencies, recovery priorities, and the financial consequences of a business disruption.

Cybersecurity can't be reduced to buying tools. An incident affects revenue, contracts, reputation, obligations to regulators, and the board's ability to continue operating the business.

The first task isn't to protect everything equally, but to determine which processes must be restored in minutes, hours, or days. Only then do technical controls gain business meaning.

The board must regularly review disruption scenarios, authority for crisis decisions, backup availability, and the credibility of reporting.

Resilience is proven through exercise and evidence, not a statement.
