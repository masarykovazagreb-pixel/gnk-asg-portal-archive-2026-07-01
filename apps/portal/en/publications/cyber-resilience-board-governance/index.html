Cyber Resilience: Board Governance

The board must understand dependencies, recovery priorities, and the financial consequences of a business disruption.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

The board must understand dependencies, recovery priorities, and the financial consequences of a business disruption.

Cybersecurity can't be reduced to buying tools. An incident affects revenue, contracts, reputation, obligations to regulators, and the board's ability to continue operating the business.

The first task isn't to protect everything equally, but to determine which processes must be restored in minutes, hours, or days. Only then do technical controls gain business meaning.

The board must regularly review disruption scenarios, authority for crisis decisions, backup availability, and the credibility of reporting.

Resilience is proven through exercise and evidence, not a statement.

Boards that receive cybersecurity updates only after an incident occurs are, by definition, receiving oversight information too late to influence the decisions that mattered most.

Regular, structured reporting on cyber risk, comparable in rigour to financial reporting, gives boards the standing information needed to ask informed questions rather than reactive ones.

Board members without deep technical backgrounds can still ask meaningful oversight questions when reporting is structured around business impact rather than technical detail alone.

Regular briefings, even brief ones, keep cyber risk visible as a standing governance topic rather than one that surfaces only during a crisis.

Establishing a standing cyber risk agenda item, reviewed every board cycle, prevents the topic from being deprioritised until an incident forces attention.

This standing visibility also signals to the rest of the organisation that cyber risk carries genuine board-level priority, not just departmental attention.

This governance discipline also strengthens an organisation's position when engaging with insurers, regulators, or partners who increasingly expect to see evidence of active board engagement on cyber matters.
