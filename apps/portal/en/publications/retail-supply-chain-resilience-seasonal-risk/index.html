Retail Supply Chain Resilience: Seasonal Risk

A retail chain that doesn't plan seasonal demand peaks in advance ends up solving stock shortages through improvisation at the worst possible moment.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

A retail chain that doesn't plan seasonal demand peaks in advance ends up solving stock shortages through improvisation at the worst possible moment.

Seasonal demand peaks are predictable in advance, but require inventory and logistics planning months before the period itself, not a reaction once shortages already appear on shelves.

Over-reliance on a single supply source for seasonally critical items creates a risk that materialises exactly at peak demand, when alternatives are most expensive.

Analysis of historical sales data, adjusted for current market trends, provides a more precise basis for inventory planning than simply repeating last year's orders.

Flexible supplier contracts, allowing quick order adjustments within the season, reduce the risk of both excess and insufficient inventory relative to actual demand.

Building buffer capacity for predictable seasonal peaks, rather than optimising purely for average demand, reduces the disruption risk concentrated in an organisation's busiest periods.

Historical demand pattern analysis, updated annually rather than relied upon indefinitely, keeps seasonal capacity planning aligned with evolving consumer behaviour.

This annual refresh should incorporate not just historical patterns but emerging trends that could signal a genuine shift in seasonal demand behaviour.

Organisations that build genuine buffer capacity for their specific seasonal patterns, rather than applying generic industry assumptions, achieve more cost-effective resilience than either under-preparing for peak demand or over-investing in capacity that sits idle most of the year. This tailored approach requires investment in understanding an organisation's own specific demand patterns rather than relying on broader industry benchmarks alone.
