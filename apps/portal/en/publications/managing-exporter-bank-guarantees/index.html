Managing Exporter Bank Guarantees

A bank guarantee ties up a company's credit line even when it's never activated, and that hidden cost is rarely tracked systematically.

Nermin Sefić1 August 2026

A bank guarantee ties up a company's credit line even when it's never activated, and that hidden cost is rarely tracked systematically.

A performance or advance-payment guarantee doesn't formally represent a cost until activated, but ties up part of a company's credit line just as real debt would.

Companies holding multiple active guarantees toward different clients at once often lose track of their total tied-up exposure to banks.

A centralised registry of all issued guarantees, with expiry dates and activation conditions, enables an accurate assessment of remaining credit capacity at any moment.

Timely release of expired or no-longer-needed guarantees frees up credit space for new business without requiring additional negotiation with the bank.

Consolidating guarantee tracking across all banking relationships, not just the primary relationship bank, gives treasury an accurate picture of total tied-up credit capacity.

Regular communication with relationship banks about anticipated guarantee needs helps avoid last-minute capacity constraints during periods of high demand.

This proactive communication becomes particularly valuable during periods of rapid export growth, when guarantee needs can escalate faster than informal tracking anticipates.

Organisations with mature guarantee management practices typically maintain a rolling forecast of anticipated future needs, shared proactively with banking partners well before those needs become urgent. This forward visibility strengthens the banking relationship while ensuring capacity is available precisely when required.

This forward visibility strengthens banking relationships considerably more than reactive, last-minute requests.

Consolidated tracking across all banking relationships remains the clearest path to understanding total exposure at any moment.
