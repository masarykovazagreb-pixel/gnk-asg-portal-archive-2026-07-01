Supplier Due Diligence: ESG Criteria

ESG supplier assessment reduced to a questionnaire without on-site verification gives a false sense of security, not a real picture of risk.

Nermin Sefić2 August 2026

ESG supplier assessment reduced to a questionnaire without on-site verification gives a false sense of security, not a real picture of risk.

Regulatory pressure and demands from large business partners increasingly force companies to document ESG practice not just for their own operations, but across their entire supplier chain.

A standard ESG questionnaire filled out by a supplier, without independent verification or a site visit, easily becomes a formality that doesn't reflect actual practice on the ground.

Concentration risk grows when ESG assessment happens once at contract signing rather than periodically — supplier practice changes, and documentation often lags behind reality.

Companies that integrate ESG due diligence into regular supplier-management processes, rather than treating it as a separate compliance task, get a more accurate picture of real risk.

Periodic on-site verification, not just questionnaire responses, distinguishes genuine ESG practice from documentation that may not reflect actual conditions in a supplier's operations.

Integrating ESG assessment into regular supplier review cycles, rather than a separate annual exercise, keeps the organisation's picture of supplier practice genuinely current.

This integration also distributes the assessment workload more evenly throughout the year, rather than concentrating it into a single, resource-intensive annual push.

Organisations that build genuine verification capability into their ESG due diligence process, rather than relying entirely on supplier self-reporting, develop a considerably more accurate picture of actual practice across their supply chain. This verification investment increasingly matters as stakeholders scrutinise ESG claims with growing sophistication and scepticism toward unverified assertions.
