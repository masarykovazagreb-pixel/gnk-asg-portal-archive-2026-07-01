Parameters and analyses: the 64 parameters SPT measures — and what they show

From a metre of running to a gram of protein: SPT measures 64 parameters in 11 groups and builds six analyses from them for the staff's daily decisions. Here they all are — with the unit, the data source, what each one shows and the features that use them.

AuthorNermin SefićAuthor profileGNK ASG · GNK DINAMO Ltd. USA Group

Sports staff today don't lack data — they lack clear answers. That is why SPT (Sports Performance Tracking) doesn't count everything that can be counted; it measures 64 parameters, each with a clear purpose: external load (what the athlete did), internal response (how the body coped), sleep and recovery, body composition, nutrition and hydration, field tests, wellness and tactics.

Below is the complete list — by group, with the unit or method, the data source and an explanation of what each parameter shows. It is followed by an example of how the parameters combine, the six analyses built from them every day, the key features with what they do and why, and an overview of every feature, device and technical specification.

In brief

  • 64 parameters in 11 groups: ten groups of six and tactics with four
  • 6 analyses for daily decisions — from morning readiness to the return after injury
  • More than 50 modules in 8 feature groups, sharing one athlete record
  • Data sources: SPT Pod (20 Hz GNSS, about ±10 cm UWB, 1000 Hz IMU), SPT watch, chest strap, smart scale, the app and field tests
  • Tailored thresholds: adjusted per club, sport, position, age and athlete — for athletes aged 12 and up
  • Wellness and sports analytics — not a medical diagnosis

1. Movement and speed

Source: SPT Pod · 20 Hz GNSS / UWB · parameters 1–6

External load — what the athlete actually did. The SPT Pod records position 20 times per second outdoors (GNSS) and to about ±10 cm indoors (UWB), so distance, speed and sprints are measured the same way on grass and on court.

#ParameterMeasured asWhat it shows
1Total distancemThe volume of a session or match, compared with the target for that day of the microcycle and the positional average.
2Distance by speed zonem · Z1–Z5The intensity profile: how long versus how fast the session was — the same kilometres can be easy or exhausting.
3High-speed running (HSR)m · > 19.8 km/hExposure to high intensity; sudden HSR spikes are associated with hamstring injuries.
4Sprintscount · m > 25 km/hRepeated-sprint ability; fewer sprints in the second half is a clear sign of fatigue.
5Peak speedkm/h · % of personal bestIf an athlete does not reach about 90% of their maximum for weeks, they lose the “vaccine” against sprint injuries.
6Metres per minutem/minRelative intensity, comparable across matches and sessions of different length and players with different minutes.

2. Mechanical load

Source: SPT Pod · 1000 Hz IMU · parameters 7–12

What GPS alone cannot see well enough: sharp accelerations, braking, changes of direction, jumps and contacts. The 1000 Hz inertial sensor captures events lasting milliseconds — exactly the ones that load muscles, tendons and joints the most.

#ParameterMeasured asWhat it shows
7Accelerationscount · > 3 m/s²Explosive demands on the thigh and calf muscles; GPS alone does not see them precisely enough.
8Decelerationscount · < −3 m/s²Eccentric load — the most mechanically demanding part of play for tendons and joints.
9Changes of directioncount · angleLoad on the knees, groin and ankles; key in football, basketball, handball and tennis.
10Accelerometer loadAUThe sum of all body movement — it measures load even where GPS cannot see: indoors, in duels, in the gym.
11Impacts and collisionscount · gContacts, falls and impacts above a set threshold; in contact sports a signal to check on the player.
12Jumps and landingscount · cm · gJump volume and landing hardness; in volleyball and basketball the main driver of patellar-tendon overload.

3. Heart and cardiovascular response

Source: chest strap · SPT watch · parameters 13–18

Internal load — how the body experienced the session. The chest strap delivers beat-by-beat heart rate and the watch tracks resting heart rate and HRV; the same external numbers cost a rested and a tired athlete very different amounts.

#ParameterMeasured asWhat it shows
13Live heart rate and % HRmaxbpm · %Internal intensity in real time; the staff sees who is in the red zone while play is on.
14Time in heart-rate zonesmin · Z1–Z5Whether the session hit the intended stimulus: aerobic base, threshold or high intensity.
15TRIMP (heart-rate load)AUHow the body experienced the session — the same external work is easy for a rested athlete and hard for a tired one.
16Heart-rate recovery (HRR)bpm in 60 sHow fast heart rate drops after effort; slower recovery warns of fatigue or the onset of illness.
17Resting heart ratebpmA morning rise of 5 or more beats often precedes illness, overreaching or poor sleep.
18HRV (RMSSD)msAutonomic nervous system balance — the most sensitive non-invasive readiness marker; what matters is deviation from the personal norm.

4. Sleep and recovery

Source: SPT watch · parameters 19–24

Recovery happens at night. The watch measures sleep duration and stages, awakenings, breathing and skin temperature, and the 0–100 morning readiness index turns it all into one decision: full intensity or a recovery plan.

#ParameterMeasured asWhat it shows
19Sleep durationhLess than 7 hours of sleep is associated with higher injury risk and slower reactions.
20Sleep stagesdeep · REM · lightDeep sleep drives physical recovery, REM learning and concentration; late matches shift the stages.
21Sleep efficiency and awakenings% · countQuality, not just quantity: fragmented sleep reduces recovery even after 8 hours in bed.
22Breathing rate in sleepbreaths/minA rise in breathing rate is one of the earliest signs of infection.
23Skin temperaturedeviation °CDeviation from the personal norm flags inflammation or illness; for female athletes it also tracks cycle phase.
24Readiness index0–100The morning composite (HRV, resting heart rate, sleep, questionnaire): green trains at full intensity, red gets a recovery plan.

5. Body composition

Source: SPT scale · bioimpedance · parameters 25–30

Every morning the smart scale uses bioimpedance to record mass, fat and muscle, water, visceral fat and basal metabolic rate. The trend over weeks says more than a single weigh-in — and it is the basis of the nutrition plan.

#ParameterMeasured asWhat it shows
25Body masskgThe daily trend; a sudden drop warns of dehydration or insufficient energy intake.
26Body fat percentage%Composition against the target for the position and season phase; the trend matters, not a single day.
27Muscle masskgMuscle loss during injury or a break — the basis of the nutrition and strength plan for the return.
28Total body water%Hydration and recovery; together with morning mass it gives a more reliable picture than weight alone.
29Visceral fatlevelA health marker — especially relevant for recreational athletes and veterans.
30Basal metabolic ratekcal/dayThe basis for calculating daily energy needs and the nutrition plan.

6. Nutrition and energy

Source: app · scale · sensors · parameters 31–36

Energy in and out: what the athlete ate and how much they used. From that SPT calculates energy availability, protein and carbohydrate per kilogram and meal timing — so load and nutrition are planned together.

#ParameterMeasured asWhat it shows
31Energy intakekcalWhat the athlete ate — a meal photo, the food database or the nutritionist's plan.
32Energy expenditurekcalCalculated from heart rate, accelerometer and basal metabolic rate: how much was actually used in training and across the day.
33Energy availabilitykcal/kg fat-free massBelow about 30 kcal per kg of fat-free mass the risk of RED-S rises: hormonal disruption, weaker bones, more injuries.
34Proteing/kgFor muscle recovery typically 1.6–2.2 g per kg of body mass, spread across 4–5 meals.
35Carbohydratesg/kgFuel for intensity; intake is periodised to load — more the day before a match, less on recovery days.
36Meal timingmin after effortProtein and carbohydrate within about 60 minutes of effort speed up recovery; the app sends a reminder.

38Hydration status% · urine colourJust 2% dehydration measurably reduces endurance, speed and concentration.

8. Load and injury risk

Source: SPT analytics · AI · parameters 43–48

This is where numbers become decisions. ACWR, monotony, session RPE, stride asymmetry and the AI risk estimate show who is entering the risk zone — early enough for the staff to act before an injury.

#ParameterMeasured asWhat it shows
43ACWR (acute : chronic)ratio · EWMAThe load of the last 7 days against the 28-day average; about 0.8–1.3 is balanced, above 1.5 risky.
44Monotony and strainindexHow similar the days of the week are and how large the total stress is — a uniformly heavy week precedes illness and overtraining.
45sRPE (session RPE)AUEffort rating × minutes; internal load even without sensors — for youth and recreational athletes.
46Stride asymmetry L/R%A left–right difference above about 10% warns of compensation, an early injury or incomplete recovery.

48Return-to-play index (RTP)% of personal normAfter injury it compares speed, accelerations and load with pre-injury values — an objective return criterion.

How the parameters combine

A single number is rarely enough. External load (what the athlete did) only tells you what a session really cost when it is combined with the internal response (how the body coped) and recovery (sleep, HRV, wellness).

A real-world example: two midfielders each cover 10.2 km with 640 m of high-speed running. For the first, heart rate stays mostly in zone 3, HRV is stable and he slept eight hours — green. The second has a TRIMP a quarter higher, HRV 15% below his personal norm and six hours of sleep — yellow. Same session, different cost: tomorrow the second one gets an adjusted plan.

That is why SPT keeps a personal norm for every parameter (the average of recent weeks) and compares it with the position and the league profile. A deviation from one's own norm is often more important than the absolute value.

What the analyses show

Parameters are the raw material; the value appears when they are combined. From them SPT automatically builds six analyses that the staff, the athlete and partners use every day. Each one lists when it is used, who reads it and which parameters feed it.

1. Morning readiness

Who can go full intensity today and who needs an adjusted session: a green/amber/red traffic light for the whole squad before the first session.

When and who: every morning before the first session — head coach and strength & conditioning coach.

Input parameters: 17 Resting heart rate · 18 HRV (RMSSD) · 19 Sleep duration · 21 Sleep efficiency and awakenings · 23 Skin temperature · 24 Readiness index · 43 ACWR (acute : chronic) · 55 Fatigue · 56 Muscle soreness · 57 Perceived sleep quality · 58 Stress · 59 Mood and motivation

2. Session report

Whether the session hit its goal: planned versus delivered load per player and position, with deviations highlighted.

When and who: right after the session or match — coach, S&C coach and analyst.

Input parameters: 1 Total distance · 3 High-speed running (HSR) · 4 Sprints · 5 Peak speed · 6 Metres per minute · 7 Accelerations · 8 Decelerations · 10 Accelerometer load · 14 Time in heart-rate zones · 15 TRIMP (heart-rate load) · 45 sRPE (session RPE)

3. Weekly risk

Who is entering the risk zone: ACWR, monotony, asymmetry and the AI estimate, with reasons and a recommendation for next week.

When and who: at the end of the week and when planning the microcycle — staff and medical team.

Input parameters: 3 High-speed running (HSR) · 8 Decelerations · 18 HRV (RMSSD) · 43 ACWR (acute : chronic) · 44 Monotony and strain · 45 sRPE (session RPE) · 46 Stride asymmetry L/R · 47 AI injury-risk estimate · 56 Muscle soreness

4. Nutrition versus load

Whether intake matched expenditure: energy availability, protein and carbohydrate by microcycle day, with a meal and hydration plan.

When and who: daily and weekly — nutritionist, S&C coach and the athlete.

5. Benchmarking and development

How a player compares with their own norm, position and league profile; the season trend for scouting and youth development.

When and who: monthly and seasonally — scout, academy lead and the athlete.

When a return is safe: the RTP index compares current values with pre-injury values, step by step.

Key features: what they do and why

Behind every number in SPT is a method from sports science. These are the most important ones.

ACWR

Load that builds, not breaks

What it does: For every athlete SPT calculates the ratio of acute (7-day) to chronic (28-day) load from GPS metrics and perceived exertion (sRPE × minutes).

Why it matters: Sudden load spikes are associated with a higher risk of soft-tissue injuries. A zone of roughly 0.8–1.3 is considered the 'sweet spot', while values above ~1.5 call for caution (Gabbett, British Journal of Sports Medicine, 2016). SPT colours every player green, amber or red — before training, not after an injury.

AI

AI injury-risk prediction

What it does: The model combines ACWR, daily readiness, wellness, stride asymmetry, injury history and age into a single risk score from 0 to 100 and lists the main contributing factors.

Why it matters: An injury is rarely caused by one number. Combining signals gives an early warning with an explanation — the staff decides, the model assists. SPT is a decision-support tool, not a medical diagnosis.

GPS + UWB

Hybrid positioning: GPS outdoors, UWB indoors

What it does: Outdoors the sensor uses multiple satellite systems at 20 Hz; indoors UWB anchors measure the time of flight of radio pulses and deliver positions with roughly ±10 cm accuracy. AUTO mode picks the source by itself.

Why it matters: Indoor sports make up half of the sporting world, and GPS does not work under a roof. The same metric outdoors and indoors means comparable data across the whole season.

BIOMECH

Asymmetry that signals a problem

What it does: The IMU measures the ground contact time of the left and right foot (GCT) and countermovement jump (CMJ) height over time.

Why it matters: Rising left–right asymmetry and a drop in CMJ often precede overload. SPT raises a flag when asymmetry exceeds 6 % — time for the physiotherapist, not another sprint.

RTP

Return to play in 6 steps

What it does: Every injury gets a protocol: pain control → range of motion → strength → running → sport-specific work → full training. Each step has a criterion and a physiotherapist's sign-off.

Why it matters: Returning too early is a common cause of re-injury. Criteria instead of the calendar — and the whole staff sees the same status.

READY

Readiness 0–100 every morning

What it does: Sleep, HRV, resting heart rate, muscle soreness, stress and mood combine into one number with a traffic light.

Why it matters: The same session for a tired and a rested player is not the same session. Readiness sets the dose of the day.

CYCLE

Training adapted to the cycle

What it does: Female athletes log their cycle phase and SPT gives phase-specific recommendations: volume, intensity, recovery and nutrition.

Why it matters: Hormonal changes affect thermoregulation, recovery and perceived exertion. Individualisation instead of averages.

Who SPT is for

The same data, different roles: everyone sees what they need — and nothing they shouldn't.

  • Clubs — One source of truth for the first team, youth categories and the medical department. League comparison, board reports and data that stays at the club when people leave.
  • Coaches — A live picture of the pitch, minutes managed by risk, player comparison and a post-match report — without spreadsheets.
  • S&C coaches — ACWR, monotony, zones, sprints and the dose for each day of the microcycle. Plan, measure, adapt.
  • Players — A personal record, readiness every morning, goals and progress. Your data, your career.
  • Scouts — Objective physical metrics alongside video: shortlists with ratings, percentiles by position and age.
  • Amateur athletes — Watch and app: heart rate, sleep, recovery and a training plan that adapts to your day.
  • Academies & youth 12+ — Age curves, growth and load monitoring, parental consent and protection of minors' data.
  • Manufacturers (OEM) — License SPT software, tools and servers for your own hardware — under your brand or ours.

Constant development and partners

SPT software is bought and integrated by other manufacturers of similar athlete-tracking systems. It is implemented in their products — sometimes under their brand, but always with our tools, our software and our servers. And we keep developing it every day.

  • Firmware SDK and protocol — A ready-made software stack for sensors: GNSS/IMU fusion, UWB ranging, BLE synchronisation and OTA updates.
  • Cloud and edge servers — Real-time ingestion, processing and storage on SPT infrastructure — or on yours.
  • White-label app — Your logo, colours, domain and app-store listings; our code and our updates.
