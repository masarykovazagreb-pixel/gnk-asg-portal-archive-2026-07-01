Market Crises

Four major breaks in world markets over the past quarter-century — stocks, oil, gold and food. Figures are rounded, but grounded in publicly documented peaks and troughs of each period.

The Dot-com Crash

March 2000 – October 2002

A wave of investment in unprofitable internet companies inflated the Nasdaq to an unsustainable level. When sentiment turned, the decline lasted nearly 30 months and erased most of the tech sector's market capitalisation.

The Global Financial Crisis

October 2007 – March 2009

The collapse of the US subprime mortgage market grew into a global banking crisis. Oil experienced one of the most dramatic reversals in history in the same period — from a record peak to a deep trough in just five months.

The COVID-19 Crash and Negative Oil

February – April 2020

The fastest bear market in history — the S&P 500 lost a third of its value in just over a month. A month later came a genuinely unprecedented event: the WTI front-month contract briefly traded below zero, as storage capacity ran out for the oil glut. Gold hit record highs in the same crisis period as a safe haven.

Inflation Shock, War and Food

2022

Rapid monetary tightening to curb inflation triggered one of the decade's longer bear markets. In parallel, the disruption of Black Sea grain exports following Russia's invasion of Ukraine sent wheat and corn prices to multi-year highs, directly hitting world food prices.
