Thought as input · Clinical trials

Thought as a keyboard

“Thought as input” means one thing: that a thought becomes an input. Not in the sense of reading everything someone thinks, but a narrow, agreed signal — a sentence the person chooses to say inside their head. In 2025 it was shown that such a signal can be decoded, in people who have lost speech.

74%accuracy reading inner speech on a 125,000-word vocabulary

What has actually been measured

The study published in Cell on 14 August 2025 involved four participants with electrodes in the motor cortex (ALS or brainstem stroke). The system decoded imagined sentences from a 125,000-word vocabulary with up to 74% accuracy. Error rates were 26–54% for the large vocabulary and 14–33% for a 50-word vocabulary. 1112

The same study showed that privacy is solved not by a promise but by a mechanism: decoding starts only when the user “says” a chosen keyword inside their head. The system recognised the keyword with more than 98% accuracy, and without it inner speech did not come out. 1112

By June 2026 a long-term result was published: one participant with ALS used the system at home for more than 3,800 hours over 19 months, produced 183,060 sentences (1,960,163 words) at an average of 56 words per minute, and held full-time employment. In controlled tests word accuracy exceeded 99% on a 125,000-word vocabulary. 15

In March 2026 another team demonstrated typing by thought on a virtual keyboard. A participant with a spinal cord injury typed up to 110 characters, or 22 words per minute, at 95% accuracy and a 1.6% word error rate — about 81% of the speed at which an average person of his age texts on a phone (27.3 words per minute). 13

Latency fell at the same time: in 2025 a system was published that turns brain signals into sound in 25 milliseconds — about the delay of your own voice — and can vary intonation and sing. 910

What it is not

This is not a consumer product and will not be one soon. It requires brain surgery, electrodes and teams to calibrate it; participants are few and each case is its own. What is genuinely new is that inner speech has been shown to exist as a readable signal — the foundation everything else builds on.

What would have to be true

No wire through the skull, no daily calibration, and proof that the signal stays stable for years. Until then: clinic only.

Medicine · Before regulators

A vaccine written for one person

A personalised mRNA vaccine does not sit on a shelf. For each patient their own tumour is sequenced, up to 34 of its neoantigens are chosen, and only then is a dose manufactured. That logistics was the real test — and in 2026 it passed phase 3.

−49%lower risk of melanoma recurrence at five years (HR 0.51)

What has actually been measured

On 19 August 2026 Merck and Moderna reported that phase 3 (INTerpath-001, 1,137 patients with completely resected stage IIB–IV melanoma) met the primary endpoint — recurrence-free survival — and the key secondary endpoint, distant metastasis-free survival. It is the first positive phase 3 result for a personalised mRNA cancer therapy. 1819

Five-year phase 2b data (KEYNOTE-942, 157 patients) showed a 49% lower risk of recurrence or death (HR 0.51; 95% CI 0.294–0.887) and a 59% lower risk of distant metastasis (HR 0.411; 95% CI 0.200–0.843). At five years, 68.8% of the combination arm and 49.1% of the immunotherapy-only arm were cancer-free. 1617

As of 21 October 2026 the therapy is still investigational: it has no regulatory approval and is not used outside clinical trials and expanded-access programmes. 19

What it is not

“Cancer vaccine” sounds like a cure, but this is adjuvant treatment: given after surgery so the disease does not return. The numbers are strong but measured in one tumour type (melanoma) and without full phase 3 data published yet — a press release is not a peer-reviewed paper.

What would have to be true

Three things: full phase 3 data, approval, and a manufacturing chain that delivers a dose in weeks rather than months. Without the third, the vaccine stays available to very few people.

Fast learning · Available today

Fast learning isn’t a chip. It’s a loop.

The biggest learning jump of 2025 came not from neurotechnology but from pedagogy with a tool: a tutor that does not give the answer but asks the next question. Harvard measured it in a randomised trial — against the best method a classroom has.

2×more learning gain with an AI tutor than in a well-run classroom, in less time

What has actually been measured

In a randomised crossover trial with 194 physics students the median post-test score was 4.5 with the AI tutor and 3.5 in the active-learning classroom, against a shared baseline of 2.75. The difference is statistically significant (p < 10⁻⁸) and the gain is more than double; the estimated effect size is 0.73–1.3 standard deviations. 67

Learning took less time: a median of 49 minutes with the tutor, against 60 minutes of lesson time in class. Students also reported higher engagement (4.1 vs 3.6 on a 1–5 scale) and motivation (3.4 vs 3.1). 6

The key is not the model but the design: the tutor was built on the same pedagogical rules as the lesson and was not allowed to skip a step. The authors stress this themselves — the result describes a purpose-built tutor, not any chatbot a student opens alone. 6

What it is not

This is the least science-fictional and most usable thing on this page. No implant, no pill: there is a tool you can switch on today, but only if it is built as instruction, not as conversation.

What would have to be true

There is no technology to wait for. What is missing is schools measuring progress per lesson instead of time on screen.

Wireless power · Field trials

Power without wire, through air

Wireless power transfer is not “Wi-Fi for electricity”. It is a narrow beam of light or microwaves from point to point, with a receiver that turns it back into electricity. It sounds like science fiction, yet three things actually happened: in orbit, in a desert, and in China.

8.6 kmdistance over which more than 800 W was laser-beamed in 2025

What has actually been measured

In 2023 Caltech’s MAPLE demonstrator transferred power wirelessly in space and aimed it at Earth; a receiver on a laboratory roof in Pasadena detected the signal on 22 May 2023. The transmitted power was just 200 milliwatts — less than a phone camera light — and only a fraction of it reached Earth. 12

In May 2025 DARPA’s POWER programme delivered more than 800 W by laser across 8.6 km for 30 seconds, with more than one megajoule in total and over 20% receiver efficiency. The test ran point-to-point along the ground, through the thickest part of the atmosphere — a harder case than beaming upward. 58

In May 2026 China’s “Sun Chasing” project reported 20.8% DC-to-DC efficiency over 100 metres with 1,180 W delivered, and 88% beam collection. The same system powered a drone in flight: 143 W at 30 metres while flying at 30 km/h. 14

What it is not

The beam must see the receiver: a wall, a hill or a bird in the path is a problem, and safety zones are not a formality. End-to-end efficiency is still far from a copper cable. What is real: for places a cable cannot reach — an island, a mountain, a drone in flight, a satellite — this stops being an idea.

What would have to be true

What is missing is boring: safety rules, certification and a first commercial contract. The technology has been shown; the infrastructure of trust has not.

Computing · Laboratory proof

A computer that errs less as it grows

A quantum computer is not faster because it is “quantum”, but because it can approach certain problems differently. The brake is error. Twenty-five years after theory promised those errors could be corrected, 2024 showed a real chip doing it — and doing it better the larger it grew.

×2.14drop in logical-qubit error when the code grows by two steps

What has actually been measured

On the 105-qubit Willow chip Google reported in 2024 (in Nature on 27 February 2025) a surface code operating below threshold: the distance-7 code uses 101 qubits with 0.143% error per cycle, and each step up in code distance cuts the error by about 2.14 times. 34

The logical qubit lived 2.4 times longer than the best physical qubit on the chip — the “below breakeven” line, crossed in real hardware for the first time. Decoding ran in real time, with an average latency of 63 microseconds. 34

What it is not

This is not yet a useful computer. It means one thing: the worst kind of error — the one that grows with size — has stopped growing. Practical use needs thousands of times more qubits and a much lower error per operation. That is why this is news of the decade, not product of the year.

What would have to be true

The next step is not “quantum” but engineering: more qubits, less noise, and an algorithm that actually earns on top of it. Until then cryptography is preparing, computers are not yet.
