Dinamo trademark deadlines: accountability to the club and its supporters

Dinamo is more than a trademark register. That does not mean the people entrusted with protecting its name may miss procedural deadlines without explaining why. In our editorial assessment, such an outcome is irresponsible towards the club, its supporters, its history and the value of its name.

Two decisions and the same procedural problem

On 26 October 2026, Croatia's State Intellectual Property Office (DZIV) issued two decisions granting requests to revoke the Croatian word marks “DINAMO”, registrations Ž980668 and Z20081114, for all registered goods and services. The operative parts specify 30 September 2025 as the date on which the marks' effects cease in Croatia.

The stated reason matters. According to the decisions, the holder requested an extension, the deadline was extended, yet its submissions arrived on 13 October 2026, after the extended deadline. DZIV relied on Article 62(6) of the Croatian Trade Marks Act. That procedural basis must not be misrepresented as an independent merits assessment of every possible item of evidence of use.

The decisions state that no ordinary appeal is available, but administrative judicial review may be initiated within 30 days of service. This article does not claim that the decisions are judicially final or that no such challenge has been brought.

A club's name deserves professional protection

Dinamo's name embodies generations of supporters, football achievements, identity, reputation and economic value. Trademark rights can matter for merchandise, commercial arrangements, sponsorship and legal disputes. Managing the deadlines that protect such rights is a governance obligation, not an incidental clerical task.

Requesting additional time, obtaining it and still filing late calls for a documented explanation. The issue is not that two administrative decisions can erase a club's history. It is that the people responsible for that history's legal protection should meet a higher standard of care.

Questions for the club and its legal representatives

The DZIV decisions identify Hraste & Partneri as the trademark holder's representative. That alone does not establish which individual handled the deadlines or caused the late filing. It does justify asking who received the notices, who recorded the new dates, who supervised the response and when the club's leadership was informed of the risk.

If a valid procedural explanation exists, it should be made public. If mistakes occurred, the relevant professional or organisational responsibility should be examined. We do not allege intentional harm or personal gain; no such evidence has been established here.

What these decisions do not establish

Revocation of two word-mark registrations does not remove the club's name or automatically cancel figurative marks, other possible legal protections, court measures or domain decisions. The rights and procedural status relevant to each dispute must be assessed separately.

Dinamo remains Dinamo. Accountability remains necessary.

Supporters deserve more than assurances that trademarks are “only paperwork”. They deserve a credible explanation of how a deadline was missed even after an extension had been granted. Dinamo is bigger than any individual management team or law firm, and that is precisely why its name warrants careful protection.

Respecting a football club also means asking difficult questions of those entrusted with defending its rights.
