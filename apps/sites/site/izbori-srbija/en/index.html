Terms, without fog

Ten terms that decide how a story reads. Each definition is short on purpose.

Voter register

The list of people entitled to vote. Registered is not the same as turned out — which is why the desk does not derive turnout from the register.

Constituency

The area that elects a set number of seats. Boundaries change the value of a vote and are therefore often disputed.

Threshold

The minimum share of the vote a list needs to enter parliament. The threshold is a legal category and has changed; no figure appears here without a source.

Seat

One seat in parliament. Seats are allocated by list order, so the order of candidates is itself a political decision.

Election silence

The period in which campaign content and polls may not be published. Its start and end are set by law, not by the newsroom.

Turnout

The share of voters who cast a ballot. Until electoral bodies publish it, any figure is an estimate — and estimates are not shown as results.

Observers

Domestic and international observers watching polling stations. Their reports are a source, but not a decision by the electoral body.

Electoral fraud

Acts that breach electoral procedure. A fraud claim requires evidence; on this desk an unsourced claim does not enter.

Coalition

An alliance of lists or parties running together. A coalition vote is divided by internal agreement, not by the voter’s preference.

Monitoring desk

This format: it does not poll, forecast or rewrite the news — it records what was published, where and when, and links to the original.

Questions that keep coming back

Is this an election forecast?

No. The desk counts media items. It does not report polls as results, estimate turnout, or predict a winner.

How often is it updated?

Whenever the editions archive refreshes — usually every two hours. The page is built from the same files, so the figures cannot drift from the archive.
