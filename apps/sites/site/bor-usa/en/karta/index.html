Fight card

How to read a fight card, in order

A card is not a list of names. It is a series of contracts stating the division, the number of rounds, the ruleset and the weigh-in deadline. Read that and you can judge an event before the first round.

01Five things a card must state

A good card states the division, the number of rounds, the ruleset, the weigh-in date and the commission. A bad card states only names and nicknames. The difference is not aesthetic: without those five facts you cannot know whether the bout is even legal in that state.

02Weight classes under the Unified Rules

The Unified Rules of MMA define eight men’s divisions; in practice there are four women’s divisions. The number is a limit, not an average — a fighter must be at or below it at the weigh-in.

  • Fly 125 lb · Bantam 135 lb · Feather 145 lb · Light 155 lb
  • Welter 170 lb · Middle 185 lb · Light heavy 205 lb · Heavy 265 lb
  • Women: strawweight 115 lb · fly 125 lb · bantam 135 lb · feather 145 lb

03What the card does not say

The card does not say what a fighter earned, what the contract says, or how much weight was cut. That is not always published, and when it is, it comes from a commission or court documents — not from a promoter’s release.

Questions that keep coming back

Why does the main event have five rounds?

Because that is what was contracted and sanctioned. The rules allow five rounds for a main event, but each bout can have its own number — and it must be stated.

What does “catchweight” mean?

An agreed weight between two divisions. The bout is then not in a standard division, and that must be clear on the card and in the sanction.
