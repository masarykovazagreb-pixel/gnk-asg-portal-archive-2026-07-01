BOR USA — combat sports in America, without the noise

Boxing and MMA in the United States are not run by one organisation but by some fifty state commissions. Understand that and you understand why the same bout is judged differently in two states — and why the most important person in the arena is often the physician, not the promoter.

How BOR USA is made

Three rules: no figure without a source, no claim about a person without a document, and no announcement that cannot be checked. The rest is editing.

01Why this desk exists

Big outlets cover the result and the money. Almost nobody covers who sanctioned the bout, under which rules, how long the medical suspension lasted, or whether the fighter even held a valid licence. Those are checkable facts — and they explain why a career stops.

So BOR USA carries no announcements and no rumours. It carries rules, procedure and sources — and then shows how a single bout reads through them.

02One bout, three decisions

Before two people step into a cage, someone must license the fighters, someone must sanction the event and someone must confirm the medicals were done. In the US that is normally the state athletic commission, not a national federation.

That is why the same fighter can be suspended in one state and compete in another, if the second commission licenses him under its rules. It is not a scandal but a consequence of the structure — which is why “under whose rules” is always the first question.

Terms, without fog

Ten terms that decide how a story reads. Each definition is short on purpose.

Fight

One bout on a card; it has its own number of rounds, weight class and ruleset.

Fight card

The list of every bout on an event, from main event to prelims.

Weigh-in

The official weight check; missing the limit costs part of the purse or the bout.

Catchweight

An agreed weight between two divisions when the fighters cannot meet in one.

No contest

The bout is voided for a foul or an accidental injury; no win is recorded.

Medical suspension

A mandatory layoff after a heavy knockout or injury, set by the commission’s physician.

Commission

The state body that licenses fighters, promoters and referees, and sanctions the event.

Unified Rules

The MMA ruleset adopted by states, with local amendments.

Purse

The money for a bout, often split into guaranteed and win money.

Weight management

Planning the division and rehydration; regulated by commissions, not the promoter.

Questions that keep coming back

Is there a federation that governs MMA in the US?

No. MMA is regulated by state commissions, and the unified rules are published by their association. Promoters are private companies that need approval in each state where they stage an event.

Why does weight matter more than names?

Because the division sets the ruleset, the number of rounds and the medical requirements. A fighter who misses the limit loses part of the purse or the whole bout, however famous he is.
