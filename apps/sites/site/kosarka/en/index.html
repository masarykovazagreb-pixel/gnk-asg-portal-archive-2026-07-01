01Three levels of the same question

You can read a game as a story, as a set of numbers, or as a system of rules. The first is fun, the second is precise, the third explains. Here all three sit together: before any analysis we state what was measured and under which rules.

That is the difference between “he played well” and “he scored 12 on 8 shots with two offensive rebounds”. The first sentence cannot be checked, the second can.

  • Rules: FIBA and the NBA are not the same sport by measurement
  • Schedule: what 82 games mean and why travel changes numbers
  • Space: why five shooters change the paint
  • Numbers: what efficiency measures, and what pace does

02Why the regional league is not small

The ABA League joins clubs from several countries into one competition. For a player that means a different rhythm: fewer games, more bus travel, a different crowd and a different pressure than in a league with a rich television deal.

So comparing players across leagues without context (minutes, pace, role) is not a comparison. That is not an opinion, it is method.

Terms, without fog

Ten terms that decide how a story reads. Each definition is short on purpose.

Regular season

The part of the season that accumulates wins and losses before the playoffs.

Playoffs

The elimination stage; a series is played to a set number of wins.

Play-in

An extra round deciding the last playoff places.

Possession

One attack, from gaining the ball to a shot or a turnover.

Shooting efficiency

Points per attempt; a better measure than raw percentage.

Pace

Possessions per game; it explains why some numbers are higher without better play.

Five-out offence

A setup where all five players threaten the shot, leaving the paint open.

Rotation

The list of players who actually play and how their minutes are distributed.

Handoff

A hand-to-hand pass with a screen; the most common modern offensive pattern.

Dunk

Putting the ball through the rim from above; no special value, it counts two.

Questions that keep coming back

Why do FIBA and the NBA use different measurements?

Because the rulesets grew up separately. FIBA plays on a 28 × 15 m court with a 6.75 m line; the NBA on 28.65 × 15.24 m with a 7.24 m line. Games last 4 × 10 minutes in FIBA and 4 × 12 in the NBA.

Is 82 games a rule?

It is the standard NBA regular-season format. Shortened seasons exist, and per-game numbers cannot be compared with 82-game seasons without saying so.
