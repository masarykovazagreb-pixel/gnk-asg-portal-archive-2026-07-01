# Croatia / EU Banking, Payments and Product Benchmark Review

**Research snapshot: 11 October 2026** · **Scope: Croatia/EU, with selected international/offshore examples** · **Research aid only — not legal, tax, investment, security or regulatory advice**

> **DEMO DESKTOP ADRIA BANK** — This review accompanies a fictional, local-only desktop rehearsal. The app is not a bank, payment or e-money institution, lender, card issuer, FX dealer, exchange, crypto-asset service provider, partner, or licensed product. Its onboarding journey uses only fixed fictional choices; it does not accept real personal data or documents, verify identity, screen sanctions/PEPs, open accounts, authorize cards, transmit payments, trade, custody assets, or contact regulators.

## Executive summary

1. **Compare the legal entity and the product—not the brand.** A bank deposit, payment-institution balance and e-money balance can look similar in an app but have different legal treatment and failure protections. A local IBAN, card, “account” label, or group brand does not answer who owes the customer money or which compensation/safeguarding rules apply.
2. **The strongest design lesson is visible transparency:** show the contracting entity, product type, currencies, fees, FX assumptions, delivery estimate, cancellation limits, safeguarding/deposit-protection explanation and complaint path before a user commits.
3. **Digital-first onboarding is not “no checks.”** Provider flows publicly describe collecting identity information or documents, with additional checks varying by residence, product, transaction and risk. EBA remote-onboarding guidance applies to institutions in scope. This demo intentionally simulates branches without reproducing an identity check or collecting evidence.
4. **Offline service remains a product requirement:** branch/cash/ATM access, a human route for exceptions, accessible self-service and a clear incident/outage plan are complements to—not substitutes for—a secure app. This local browser/Electron scenario is not a core-banking platform or an operational-resilience test.
5. **“Better than all banks” is not a testable claim.** Benchmark against a defined Croatian customer segment and measured outcomes (task completion, total fee, FX spread, error rate, accessibility, support response and complaint resolution), not marketing slogans or a handful of brands.

This is a focused product comparison, not a census of every online/offline bank, offshore jurisdiction, licence, fee or regulatory rule. Provider statements describe the relevant page/agreement at access, not a guarantee of present eligibility, price, performance, coverage or service to every Croatian customer. Prices are dynamic and are deliberately not presented as directly comparable quotes.

**Protection shorthand:** Croatian credit-institution deposits are generally covered by HAOD up to €100,000 per depositor per institution, subject to the statutory rules. A bank in another Member State may use its home-state scheme. A payment institution or EMI instead safeguards relevant customer funds under its rules; that does not make its account a deposit or provide DGS cover. Scheme ceilings are not a promise that every balance, person, business, currency or account qualifies.

## 2. Online and offline service review

### Online / mobile / desktop

A strong digital service typically aims to combine:

- clear legal entity/product disclosures, accessible fee previews and a receipt explaining FX rate, markup, fees, date/time and delivery estimate;
- secure sign-in and step-up/strong customer authentication for sensitive actions, device/session management, transaction alerts and explicit consent controls;
- card freeze/unfreeze, virtual-card controls, configurable limits and easy dispute/chargeback initiation;
- instant-payment status, confirmation-of-payee/beneficiary checks where required, cancellation/recall guidance and clear pending/failed states;
- multilingual, accessible statements and support with a documented escalation and complaint path;
- user-controlled open-banking permissions and a way to see/revoke each consent;
- low-connectivity and outage messaging that does not pretend an instruction was sent or settled.

The desktop demo already demonstrates local account operations, maker/checker queues, card controls, virtual/prepaid cards, receipts, complaints and nine languages. It does not have real authentication, external payment connectivity, a production core, verified payees, dispute network, secure customer tenancy or durable back-end audit. Local browser storage is editable and is not production-grade evidence or access control.

### Offline / assisted service

Offline banking is not simply “an app without Wi-Fi.” A real institution may need accessible branches, cash and ATM services, call-centre/human support, paper or assisted alternatives, card-loss procedures, outage communications, fraud escalation, reconciliation and tested business-continuity arrangements. Teller and ATM activity needs physical security, dual control, cash transport, counterfeit controls, limits and daily balancing. Any deferred/offline card authorization is a separate scheme/processor capability with risk, limit and reconciliation implications—not a safe default to imitate in this demo.

The app’s browser/server and Electron sources are local-first for synthetic training data, while optional public FX/reference fetches need internet access. That is an implementation convenience, **not** certified offline banking, DORA compliance, disaster recovery or resilience.

## 3. Product features worth prioritizing

The following is a product roadmap, not a promise that every service can lawfully or safely be launched by one entity.

### P0 — improve the safe demonstration

1. Keep onboarding as a true, keyboard-accessible stepper with a review queue, status history, manual-review/unavailable branches and clear “no real identity check/account” copy. **Implemented as a local rehearsal.**
2. Show, by synthetic product, “who provides this?”, “bank deposit or e-money/payment balance?”, “what protection is simulated?”, and why no real protection is offered by this demo.
3. Extend fee transparency into an illustrative total-cost calculator with a timestamp/source, reference-vs-quote warning, spread/fee breakdown and no external order submission.
4. Add synthetic beneficiary-name/IBAN mismatch and instant-payment scam rehearsal, with a pause/confirm/stop screen; do not query real accounts.
5. Improve local complaint/dispute tracking, status notifications, document-free evidence categories, accessibility controls and downloadable, explicitly watermarked synthetic receipts.
6. Provide privacy/security UX: clear local-data reset, synthetic seed explanation, visible training banner, masked IDs, no real PAN/CVV, and no sensitive free-text form fields in onboarding.

### P1 — worthwhile only after regulated architecture and permissions exist

- Strong customer authentication/passkeys, device binding/revocation, secure notification preferences and fraud reporting.
- Consent dashboard for account-information/payment-initiation integrations; use only authorised providers and a valid customer consent, with revocation and data minimization.
- Optional EUDI Wallet flow after the Croatian wallet and relying-party requirements are confirmed; it must remain voluntary where the applicable rules require choice, and cannot replace AML/CFT or product eligibility checks by itself.
- Personal and business account controls, named staff roles, dual approval, bulk payouts, beneficiary templates, statements, accounting export/API and clear business-fee schedules.
- Virtual/debit/prepaid-card inventory, merchant/online/cross-border toggles, per-card limits, single-use cards and immediate freeze; real issuance requires a licensed issuer/processor/network and applicable PCI scope.
- Savings goals/pots, term/flexible savings comparison and a credit affordability journey with the correct national disclosures and responsible-lending controls; do not imply savings deposits where the service is e-money.
- Support that joins chat/phone/branch hand-offs, accessible complaint handling, fraud recovery and a human review/appeal path.
- Currency alerts and scheduled/limit functionality only after authorized pricing/market, execution, custody and best-execution requirements have been resolved. A local alert or reference rate is not an order.

Do not prioritize lending, securities, crypto custody, investment products or “offshore” entity creation until the entity, conduct, capital, safeguarding/deposit, market-abuse, privacy and resolution implications are analyzed for each exact service. “All the best banks’ features” would mean several differently licensed businesses, not one unrestricted product toggle.

### Croatia-specific checks

- HNB maintains separate lists/registers for credit institutions and payment-service/e-money entities. A name in a register is not proof that a different group brand, product, agent, territory or activity is authorized.
- Croatian deposit coverage is administered by HAOD; for a foreign EEA bank, identify its home scheme. Do not use the Croatian limit as a universal international limit.
- HNB’s consumer-lending measure is a relevant constraint for future synthetic demos. Its caps have specified scope and exemptions; they are not a universal affordability model or an automatic approval/rejection formula.
- Croatia’s Consumer Credit Act 86/2026 effective-date note above is based on its official publication; re-check *Narodne novine* and the consolidated law on/after 20 November 2026. A law planned for that date is not a reason to ship it prematurely in a training script.
- The AMLR transition to 2027 does not suspend current Croatian/EU AML duties. Keep current national law and future EU law clearly separated in training materials.

## 5. Practical product controls for a real Croatian service

If a lawfully authorized entity were considered later, use a gated discovery process:

1. **Write the service perimeter:** customer type/residency, contracting entity, deposit/payment/e-money classification, funds flow, product, currencies, rails, partner roles and all jurisdictions.
2. **Map the rule set and permissions:** HNB/ECB/Hanfa or the competent home/host authorities; authorization, passport, product/market permissions, safeguarding/deposit treatment, complaint route and effective dates.
3. **Design disclosures before workflows:** who owes the funds; fee/spread/quote validity; expected delivery/cancellation; account protection; exchange-risk; disputes; data use; and support hours.
4. **Build secure onboarding separately:** lawful data collection and retention, remote-ID solution assessment, human/manual review, sanctions/PEP/beneficial-owner handling, accessible alternatives, rejection/explanation/appeal and secure audit trail.
5. **Separate simulation from production:** isolated test environment, synthetic identifiers, non-routable accounts, fake payment rails, no real PAN/identity data, and unambiguous print/export labels.
6. **Test people and failure states:** accessibility with disabled users; fee/rate reconciliation; payment failure/recall; fraud and complaint drills; offline branch/cash procedures; incident response; supplier outage; backup restoration; and independent security review.
7. **Measure outcomes:** compare the same corridor and customer task across dated public tariffs, then test customer comprehension, completion, errors, complaint resolution and accessibility. Do not make a superiority claim without a defined, repeatable study.
