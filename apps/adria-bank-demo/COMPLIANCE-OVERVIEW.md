# Adria Bank Demo — compliance and market-planning overview

**Snapshot date: 11 October 2026 · Planning aid only · Not legal advice**

This document explains the difference between the app's local simulation and the work that would be needed before a real bank, payment provider, lender, card issuer, FX business or crypto-asset service could operate. It is a first-pass product/compliance map, not a legal inventory, regulatory classification, application, licence, approval, or evidence of compliance. National law, supervisory expectations and product perimeters change. A qualified local firm and the relevant authorities must validate every item against the exact legal entity, product, customer, funds flow and location. For the dated Croatia/EU legal and provider comparison, public online-onboarding review, Jersey compensation-scheme update and product roadmap, see [PRODUCT-AND-COMPETITOR-REVIEW-2026-10-11.md](./PRODUCT-AND-COMPETITOR-REVIEW-2026-10-11.md).

**No regulator has been contacted. No licence or authorisation has been requested or obtained.** The ten cities in the app are proposals for research only. They are not branches, subsidiaries, launch decisions, legal entities, offices, representatives or regulated activities.

**The app has no production banking backend.** Business workflows and demo state are stored in local browser storage and can be edited or reset by a user. The local server exposes only read-only public reference-data proxies for the ECB and CBK feeds; the restricted Electron main process fetches those same public sources via IPC. Those integrations do not submit customer data or connect to banks, payment rails, identity providers or regulatory systems. A local scheduled rehearsal may run while the desktop process is alive; it does not modify application code, monitor legislation, verify licences or continue when the process is quit or the device is asleep. Regulator links are for human research, not automatic legal updates. Do not enter real customer, employee, banking, payment, identity, sanctions or confidential information.

### EU note

Croatia, Germany, France and Spain are EU Member States. A passport is **not** a substitute for a valid home-state banking authorisation, competent-supervisor notification, host-state requirements, product permissions, or local conduct and consumer rules. The consolidated Capital Requirements Directive (CRD) is a starting point for EU establishment and passport procedures, not an authorisation. Country-specific transposition and supervisory guidance must be checked for each actual structure. On 11 October 2026, the European Parliament Legislative Observatory still listed the PSD3 (2023/0209(COD)) and PSR (2023/0210(COD)) files as awaiting the Council's first-reading position; the agreed committee text is not itself an effective legal act. Check the [current procedure files](https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/procedure-file?reference=2023%2F0209%28COD%29) and Official Journal before relying on a change.

## 3. Consumer credit — Croatia and EU timing

The synthetic credit module does not make real credit decisions, calculate a legally compliant APR, produce pre-contract disclosures, sign agreements, fund loans, collect real instalments or implement consumer-credit law.

The official Croatian **Zakon o potrošačkim kreditima**, published in *Narodne novine* 86/2026, is recorded in this research snapshot as generally entering into force on **20 November 2026**. The reviewed final provision states exceptions: Article 114 paragraphs 1–6 from **1 June 2027**, and Article 3 paragraph 2 points 1–2 from **1 January 2028**. The official text and the EU Consumer Credit Directive (EU) 2023/2225 must be read together with Croatian implementing rules, transitional provisions and later guidance. Reconfirm the text and effective dates before use; these dates are a source-reading note, not legal advice.

Any real offer also needs transaction-specific analysis of the lender/distributor role, borrower type, exemptions, creditworthiness and affordability checks, advertising, pre-contract information, APR calculation, agreement form, withdrawal rights, servicing, arrears, complaints, data and record retention. Other countries may implement EU rules differently or apply separate domestic credit rules.

## 4. Cross-cutting workstreams before a real service

A regulated launch is not achieved by reproducing these screens. At minimum, a real programme would need a detailed, independently reviewed workplan covering:

1. **Legal entity, ownership and authorisation:** exact services and customer funds flow; bank vs payment/e-money/lender/crypto classification; home/host regulators; beneficial ownership; management fitness; capital, liquidity and recovery/resolution requirements; passporting or local subsidiary/branch route.
2. **Governance and people:** board accountability, risk appetite, independent compliance and internal audit, MLRO, local substance, conflicts, training, remuneration, outsourcing oversight, complaints and conduct governance.
3. **Financial crime:** enterprise ML/TF risk assessment, KYC/beneficial ownership, PEP/sanctions screening, ongoing monitoring, transaction thresholds, investigations, suspicious reporting, recordkeeping and tested controls.
4. **Payments / settlement:** direct or sponsored access, scheme rules, settlement accounts, liquidity, payee verification, sanctions, reconciliation, exception handling, returns, disputes, operational hours and outage procedures.
5. **Consumer and product protection:** clear terms and fees; creditworthiness and affordability; APR and advertising; deposit-guarantee eligibility/limits; complaints and ombudsman access; vulnerable-customer measures; accessibility obligations for covered banking services (including the European Accessibility Act from 28 June 2025, subject to scope and national implementation); marketing approvals and required language. HNB's in-scope consumer-lending criteria have applied since 1 July 2025; see the dated Croatia/EU review before modeling a credit workflow.
6. **Prudential and accounting:** audited accounting, credit-risk classification/provisioning, capital and liquidity, large exposures, stress tests, regulatory returns, tax, resolution planning and independent model validation.
7. **Technology and data:** secure system architecture, encryption/key management, identity and access, threat modelling, penetration testing, vulnerability management, software supply chain, incident response, backup/DR, retention, data-subject rights, lawful cross-border transfers and processor contracts.
8. **Crypto-specific controls if applicable:** legal token classification, authorisation, custody/segregation, private-key recovery, Travel Rule and AML, market integrity, conflicts, asset disclosure, complaints, operational controls and insolvency treatment. A static quote or synthetic token does not meet any of these requirements.
9. **Cards, cash and premises:** issuer/processor/network contracts and applicable PCI DSS controls; chargebacks; physical-security assessment, cash transport, counterfeit detection, ATM/acquirer integration, insurance and site approvals.
10. **Independent assurance and operation:** second-line challenge, internal/external audit, regulator engagement, control testing, service-level management, incident exercises and evidence that every control operates continuously.

This list is intentionally high-level and incomplete. It is a discovery aid, not an implementation plan or certification.

### FX / market-reference-data boundary

- The web server exposes read-only same-origin routes for the [ECB daily euro foreign-exchange reference rates](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/policy_and_exchange_rates/euro_reference_exchange_rates/html/index.en.html) and the [CBK foreign-exchange page](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/forex/). The Electron app uses restricted IPC fetches for those same public sources.
- Optional market-data starting point: [Twelve Data exchange-rate API documentation](https://twelvedata.com/docs). Check the provider's current availability, price, quota, display and use terms before configuring a key.
- ECB rates are daily informational references, usually published on working days, not recommended transaction rates and not executable customer quotes. CBK publishes indicative weighted-average KES rates; those are not a bank-specific offer. UAE dirham samples are derived from ECB EUR/USD with a stated USD/AED peg assumption; this is not a direct CBUAE quote.
- The optional Twelve Data integration requests only EUR/USD after a user clicks the market-data control and only when `TWELVEDATA_API_KEY` is present in the local server/Electron process environment. The key is not stored in app state or sent to the renderer; the provider response is reduced to rate, fixed symbol and timestamp. A successful result is kept in memory for 60 seconds. Review current provider terms, plan limits and licensing before use. This endpoint is market-data-only and is intentionally isolated from conversion/trade calculations.
- Feed parsers and routes are covered by local tests, but feed availability and page format can change. If a source cannot be parsed or fetched, the UI retains a labelled bundled sample with its own date. Nothing here qualifies as “real-time pricing,” a live market feed, liquidity, settlement or a connected integration.
